# Hexavalent vaccines: What can we learn from head-to-head studies?

**Markus Knuf, Hervé Haas, Pilar Garcia-Corbeira, Elisa Turriani, Piyali Mukherjee, Winnie Janssens, Valérie Berlaimont**

## 1. Introduction

In infant immunization programs, combination vaccines with the greatest number of targeted diseases are hexavalent diphtheria (D), tetanus (T), pertussis, hepatitis B virus (HBV), inactivated poliovirus (IPV) and Haemophilus influenzae type b (Hib) vaccines. Four hexavalent acellular pertussis (aP)-containing vaccines have been licensed, of which 3 are still available on the market in Europe. Two vaccines were licensed in 2000 (Hexavac and Infanrix hexa [DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib]), but the marketing authorization of Hexavac was suspended in 2005 and withdrawn in 2012 by the European Medicines Agency due to concerns about its immunogenicity against HBV. The third vaccine was licensed in 2012 outside the European Union and in 2013 in Europe (DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib, Hexaxim, Hexyon or Hexacima, Sanofi Pasteur). The fourth vaccine was approved in Europe in 2016 (DT5aP-HBV-IPV-Hib, Vaxelis, MCM Vaccine B.V.).

In this review, we present differences in composition and formulation between the 3 currently licensed hexavalent vaccines. Then, we explore the nuances of randomized head-to-head studies to critically compare their immunogenicity and safety. While studies conducted to meet registration requirements for market authorizations in different countries demonstrated the non-inferiority of immune responses induced by DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib or DT5aP-HBV-IPV-Hib versus DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib, equivalence between hexavalent vaccines was never shown.

## 2. Methods

A systematic literature search was performed on January 31, 2019 to identify published, peer-reviewed head-to-head studies comparing any of the 3 licensed hexavalent vaccines to another in terms of immunogenicity and safety. No geographical or language restrictions were applied. A second search on PubMed, Medline, Medline in Process and Embase was performed on September 24, 2020 for more recent output. The reference lists of any relevant identified systematic reviews and of the European Public Assessment Reports for the 3 vaccines were also searched for studies of interest. To ensure completeness, the Trialtrove website was searched to identify all head-to-head studies comparing ≥2 hexavalent vaccines, and completed clinical trials with published results were included. Retrieved articles were screened by 2 reviewers independently to select relevant papers based on eligibility criteria using a 2-step screening process. Discrepancies were resolved by consensus with a 3rd researcher.

A total of 12 published articles met the predefined inclusion criteria. Of these, 9 were identified during the initial literature search and 3 during the second search. One article on a post-hoc analysis in subsets of premature infants who received DT5aP-HBV-IPV-Hib or comparators in clinical trials was not further discussed because data were already analyzed in the respective studies.

A plain language summary of the findings and potential relevance and impact of this work is presented.

## 3. Results

### 3.1. Composition and formulation

The 3 authorized hexavalent vaccines were developed independently, often contain the same antigens as previous lower-valency vaccines from the same manufacturer, and none are biosimilars. The quantity of diphtheria toxoid (DT) is higher in DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib (≥30 international unit [IU], in line with the World Health Organization recommendations from 2014) compared with the other vaccines (≥20 IU). Other differences are the number (3 in DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib, 2 in DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib and 5 in DT5aP-HBV-IPV-Hib) and amount of pertussis antigens, the HBV component (recombinant Hansenula polymorpha-derived for DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib and Saccharomyces cerevisiae-derived for the other vaccines), the quantity of polyribosylribitol phosphate (PRP) of Hib and its carrier protein (tetanus toxoid in DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib and DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib, and outer membrane protein complex of Neisseria meningitidis in DT5aP-HBV-IPV-Hib) and the aluminum salts used as adjuvants. Finally, the 2 most recent vaccines have a fully-liquid formulation, while DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib must be reconstituted before administration by adding the suspension containing DTaP, HBV and IPV antigens from a pre-filled syringe to a vial containing the Hib lyophilized powder. These differences in composition and formulation could be reflected in potential differences in reactogenicity, immunogenicity and/or efficacy.

### 3.2. Immunogenicity in head-to-head studies

Immune responses induced by hexavalent vaccines were evaluated by antigen-specific seroprotection rates (correlates or surrogates), vaccine response rates, geometric mean titers (GMTs) and geometric mean concentrations (GMCs). Established correlates of protection were used to evaluate immune responses against D, T, IPV, HBV and Hib. For pertussis antigens, there are no established correlates of protection, and immune responses were evaluated either by vaccine response rates (as defined in the protocols) or percentages of participants with ≥4-fold increases in antibody titers.

In head-to-head studies, non-inferiority of the immune response to a vaccine antigen was concluded if the lower limit of the 2-sided 95% confidence interval (CI) of the difference between DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib or DT5aP-HBV-IPV-Hib versus DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib in terms of seroprotection or vaccine response rate was ≥ -10 or ≥ -5. In descriptive comparisons, differences were detected based on non-overlapping 95% CIs.

#### 3.2.1. DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib versus DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib

**3 + 1 schedule.** The systematic literature search identified 5 studies using different immunization schedules and comparing 3-dose primary vaccination with DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib versus DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib. Three studies included a booster dose administration. Infants received an HBV vaccine dose in 2 studies and Bacille Calmette-Guérin (BCG) vaccine in 1 study within 1 month after birth. Long-term persistence of immune responses was evaluated for all vaccine antigens up to 4.5 years in Colombia and for anti-HBV antibodies in 9-10-year-old children in Thailand.

*   **Immune response to D.** Non-inferiority of anti-D immune responses induced by 3-dose primary vaccination with DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib versus DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib was demonstrated in 2 studies.
    In 3 studies, no differences were detected in descriptive comparisons. In contrast, percentages of participants with anti-D antibody concentrations ≥1.0 IU/ml post-primary vaccination in Thailand (26.5% versus 12.1%) and post-booster anti-D antibody GMCs in Germany and the Czech Republic (6.08 versus 4.10 IU/mL) were higher in DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib versus DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib recipients.
    In Colombia, percentages of 4.5-year-old participants with anti-D antibody concentrations ≥0.1 IU/mL were 57.2% among children primed and boosted with DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib, 48.2% among children primed with DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib and boosted with DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib, and 59.2% among children primed with DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib and boosted with DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib.

*   **Immune response to T.** Non-inferiority of anti-T immune responses induced by 3 primary doses of DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib versus DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib was demonstrated in Colombia and Costa Rica.
    No differences were detected in descriptive analyses in 3 studies. In Thailand, percentages of participants with anti-T antibody concentrations ≥1.0 IU/ml (70.9% versus 87.9%) and anti-T antibody GMCs (1.38 versus 1.83 IU/mL) were lower postprimary vaccination for DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib versus DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib.
    In Colombia, percentages of 4.5-year-old participants with anti-T antibody concentrations ≥0.1 IU/mL were 80.8% among children primed and boosted with DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib, 76.8% among children primed with DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib and boosted with DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib, and 81.6% among children primed with DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib and boosted with DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib.

*   **Immune response to pertussis.** Non-inferiority of immune responses to pertussis toxin (PT) and filamentous hemagglutinin (FHA) induced by 3 primary doses of DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib versus DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib was demonstrated in 2 studies.
    No differences were detected in descriptive analyses in Colombia and Costa Rica. Post-primary vaccination, anti-FHA antibody GMCs in Mexico (239 versus 182 enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) unit [EU]/mL) and Thailand (148 versus 123 EU/mL) and percentages of children with ≥4-fold increases in anti-FHA antibody concentrations in Germany and the Czech Republic (89.2% versus 76.4%) were higher while anti-PT antibody GMCs in Thailand (168 versus 200 EU/mL) were lower in DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib versus DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib vaccinees. Post-booster dose of DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib versus DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib, differences were observed in percentages of children with ≥4-fold increases in anti-FHA antibody concentrations (60.4% versus 80.7%) and anti-PT antibody GMCs (112 versus 158 EU/mL) in Germany and the Czech Republic. In Mexico, differences in anti-FHA antibody GMCs (402 versus 291 EU/mL) were observed following administration of a DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib booster dose in children primed with DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib versus DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib.
    In Colombia, anti-PT and anti-FHA antibody GMCs at 4.5 years of age were similar in children primed and boosted with DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib (3.16 and 33.8 EU/mL), primed with DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib and boosted with DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib (3.13 and 35.1 EU/mL), and primed with DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib and boosted with DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib (3.06 and 27.3 EU/mL).

*   **Immune response to HBV.** Non-inferiority of anti-HBV immune responses induced by 3 primary doses of DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib versus DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib was demonstrated in 4 studies.
    While no differences were detected in descriptive analyses in 3 studies, percentages of participants with post-primary vaccination anti-HBV antibody concentrations ≥100 mIU/mL in Germany and the Czech Republic (71.9% versus 86.6%) and Mexico (91.7% versus 99.2%), and post-booster anti-HBV antibody GMCs in Germany and the Czech Republic (2140 versus 4642 mIU/mL) were lower in DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib versus DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib recipients.
    In Colombia, percentages of 4.5-year-old participants with anti-HBV antibody concentrations ≥10mIU / mL were similar among children primed and boosted with DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib (92.3%), primed with DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib and boosted with DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib (93.0%), and primed with DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib and boosted with DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib (94.4%). In Thailand, proportions of children with anti-HBV antibody concentrations ≥ 10 mIU/mL (49.3% and 42.9%) and anti-HBV antibody GMCs (13.3 versus 8.0 mIU/mL) were similar in 9- to 10-year-old children primed with DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib versus DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib. A strong anamnestic response was observed in both groups, with post-challenge seroprotection rates of 92.8% in DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib-primed and 98.7% in DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib-primed children.

*   **Immune response to poliovirus types 1, 2, 3.** Non-inferiority of immune responses to poliovirus types 1, 2 and 3 induced by 3 primary doses of DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib versus DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib was demonstrated in Colombia and Costa Rica.
    No differences were detected in descriptive analyses in Colombia and Costa Rica. Post-primary vaccination, anti-poliovirus types 1, 2 and 3 antibody GMTs in Mexico (882 versus 1370; 1655 versus 2337; 1106 versus 2186), Germany and the Czech Republic (113 versus 268; 191 versus 365; 302 versus 662) and Thailand (765 versus 1566; 1489 versus 2277; 837 versus 2029) were lower in DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib versus DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib vaccinees. Post-booster dose of DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib and DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib, differences were observed for anti-poliovirus types 1, 2 and 3 antibody GMTs (1070 versus 2696; 1858 versus 2887; 2301 versus 3902) in Germany and the Czech Republic. In Mexico, differences in anti-poliovirus type 3 antibody GMTs (6971 versus 13,337) were observed following administration of a DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib booster dose in children primed with DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib versus DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib.
    In Colombia, percentages of 4.5-year-old participants with anti-poliovirus types 1, 2 and 3 antibody titers ≥ 1:8 were similar among children primed and boosted with DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib (99.5%, 100% and 100%), primed with DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib and boosted with DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib (99.5%, 100% and 99.0%), and primed with DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib and boosted with DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib (100%, 100% and 100%).

*   **Immune response to Hib.** Non-inferiority of anti-Hib immune responses induced by 3 primary doses of DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib versus DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib was demonstrated in 3 studies.
    No differences were detected in descriptive analyses in 2 studies. Post-primary vaccination, percentages of participants with anti-PRP antibody concentrations ≥1.0 µg/mL in Germany and the Czech Republic (58.9% versus 37.2%) and Thailand (85.2% versus 71.1%), and anti-PRP antibody GMCs in Mexico (12.20 versus 6.68 µg/mL), Germany and the Czech Republic (1.19 versus 0.60 µg/mL) and Thailand (5.07 versus 2.41 µg/mL) were higher in DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib versus DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib vaccinees. Post-toddler dose, anti-PRP antibody GMCs (28.8 versus 16.5 µg/mL) were higher in DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib versus DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib recipients in Germany and the Czech Republic.
    In Colombia, percentages of 4.5-year-old participants with anti-PRP antibody concentrations ≥0.15 µg/mL and ≥1.0 µg/mL were 100% and 85.6% among children primed and boosted with DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib, 100% and 84.4% among children primed with DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib and boosted with DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib, and 100% and 90.4% among children primed with DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib and boosted with DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib.

**2 + 1 schedule.** The systematic literature search identified 1 study comparing 2 + 1 doses of DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib versus DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib administered at 3, 5 + 11–12 months of age in Sweden and Finland.
    Non-inferiority of immune responses induced by DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib versus DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib was demonstrated 1 month post-primary and -booster vaccination for all common vaccine antigens, except poliovirus types 1 and 2, for which non-inferiority criteria were not met after the 2 primary doses.
    While similar immune responses for some antigens were observed in descriptive analyses, potential differences were detected for others. Post-primary vaccination, the percentage of participants with anti-PRP antibody concentrations ≥0.15 µg/mL was higher in DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib versus DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib vaccinees (71.5% versus 57.9%). Post-booster dose, percentages of participants with anti-D antibody concentrations ≥1.0 IU/mL (82.3% versus 69.9%), anti-D antibody GMCs (1.70 versus 1.20 IU/mL), percentages of participants with anti-PRP antibody concentrations ≥1.0 µg/mL (93.5% versus 85.2%) and anti-PRP antibody GMCs (9.7 versus 5.6 µg/mL) were higher in DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib versus DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib vaccinees. In contrast, post-booster anti-PT antibody GMCs (91 versus 129 EU/mL), percentages of participants with anti-HBV antibody concentrations ≥100 mIU/mL (91.2% versus 98.0%), anti-HBV antibody GMCs (1370 versus 5015 mIU/mL), and anti-poliovirus types 1, 2 and 3 antibody GMCs (1749 versus 3279; 1729 versus 2954; 1213 versus 1906) were lower in DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib versus DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib recipients.

#### 3.2.2. DT5aP-HBV-IPV-Hib versus DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib

**3 + 1 schedule.** The systematic literature search identified 1 study comparing 3 + 1 doses of DT5aP-HBV-IPV-Hib versus DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib administered at 2, 3, 4 + 12 months of age in Finland, Germany and Belgium. Long-term persistence of immune responses was evaluated up to approximately 4 years post-vaccination.
    Non-inferiority of immune responses induced by DT5aP-HBV-IPV-Hib versus DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib was demonstrated 1 month post-primary vaccination for D, T, poliovirus types 1, 2 and 3, and Hib antigens, and post-booster dose for pertussis and HBV antigens.
    In descriptive analyses, immune responses induced by both vaccines to some antigens were similar. However, post-booster anti-D antibody GMCs (4.73 versus 3.05 IU/mL), post-primary (0.70 versus 0.53 IU/mL) and -booster (8.16 versus 3.82 IU/mL) anti-T antibody GMCs, post-booster percentages of children with anti-T antibody concentrations ≥0.1 IU/mL (100.0% versus 98.0%), and post-primary (130 versus 84 EU/mL) and –booster (197 versus 91 EU/mL) anti-PT antibody GMCs were higher in DT5aP-HBV-IPV-Hib versus DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib vaccinees. In contrast, percentages of participants with anti-FHA vaccine response post-primary (89.0% versus 96.7%) and –booster (97.2% versus 99.8%) vaccination, anti-FHA antibody GMCs post-primary (50 versus 97 EU/mL) and -booster (122 versus 197 EU/mL) vaccination, percentages of participants with anti-PRN vaccine response (86.7% versus 92.3%) and anti-PRN antibody GMCs (46.8 versus 77.8 EU/mL) post-primary vaccination were lower in DT5aP-HBV-IPV-Hib versus DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib recipients. Differences were observed between DT5aP-HBV-IPV-Hib and DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib recipients in percentages of participants with anti-PRP antibody concentrations ≥0.15 µg/mL (98.4% versus 87.0%) and ≥1.0 µg/mL (83.3% versus 36.7%), and anti-PRP antibody GMCs (3.90 versus 0.65 µg/mL) post-primary vaccination, and anti-PRP antibody GMCs (6.79 versus 21.39 µg/mL) post-booster dose.
    Approximately 4 years post-vaccination, percentages of children with anti-HBV antibody concentrations ≥10 mIU/mL were 70.2% in DT5aP-HBV-IPV-Hib and 82.0% in DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib recipients.

**2 + 1 schedule.** The systematic literature search identified 1 study comparing 2 + 1 doses of DT5aP-HBV-IPV-Hib versus DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib administered at 2, 4 + 11–12 months of age in Sweden, Italy and Finland. Long-term persistence of immune responses was evaluated up to approximately 4 years post-vaccination.
    Non-inferiority of immune responses to all common antigens induced by DT5aP-HBV-IPV-Hib versus DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib was demonstrated 1 month post-booster dose.
    While immune responses induced by both vaccines to some antigens were similar, differences were detected in descriptive analyses for others. Percentages of children with anti-D antibody titers ≥0.1 IU/mL (83.2% versus 71.6%), anti-D antibody GMCs (2.01 versus 1.46 IU/mL), percentages of children with anti-T antibody titers ≥0.1 IU/mL (98.8% versus 95.0%) and anti-T antibody GMCs (5.53 versus 3.32 IU/mL) post-booster dose, and anti-PT antibody GMCs post-primary (113 versus 92 EU/mL) and -booster (157 versus 110 EU/mL) vaccination were higher in DT5aP-HBV-IPV-Hib versus DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib vaccinees. In contrast, anti-FHA antibody GMCs post-primary (45 versus 87 EU/mL) and -booster (121 versus 204 EU/mL) vaccination, anti-PRN vaccine response rates post-primary vaccination (80.3% versus 91.6%), anti-PRN antibody GMCs post-primary (37.8 versus 78.3 EU/mL) and -booster (104.3 versus 153.5 EU/mL) vaccination, anti-HBV antibody GMCs post-primary (226 versus 343 mIU/mL) and -booster (1986 versus 3245 mIU/mL) vaccination, anti-poliovirus types 1 and 3 antibody GMTs post-primary vaccination (65 versus 88 and 77 versus 105) and anti-poliovirus type 1 antibody GMTs post-toddler dose (1707 versus 2538) were lower in DT5aP-HBV-IPV-Hib versus DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib recipients. Differences were observed between DT5aP-HBV-IPV-Hib and DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib vaccinees in percentages of participants with anti-PRP antibody concentrations ≥0.15 µg/mL (96.6% versus 77.9%) and ≥1.0 µg/mL (72.9% versus 26.7%) and anti-PRP antibody GMCs (2.38 versus 0.46 µg/mL) post-primary doses, and anti-PRP antibody GMCs (4.43 versus 7.76 µg/mL) post-toddler dose.
    Approximately 4 years post-vaccination, percentages of children with anti-HBV antibody concentrations ≥10mIU / mL were 65.7% in DT5aP-HBV-IPV-Hib and 83.7% in DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib vaccinees. Anti-PT antibody GMCs (5.3 versus 3.6 EU/mL) were higher, anti-FHA antibody GMCs (6.6 versus 11.1 EU/mL) lower, and anti-PRN antibody GMCs (5.9 versus 7.2 EU/mL) similar in DT5aP-HBV-IPV-Hib versus DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib recipients.

#### 3.2.3. 3 + 1 and 2 + 1 schedules comparisons

Although comparisons between studies should be interpreted cautiously, they offer valuable insight into immune responses induced by different vaccines and vaccination schedules. Across all vaccines, proportions of infants with seroprotective antibody levels or vaccine responses against most vaccine antigens were ≥84.0% and ≥80.3% after 3 and 2 primary doses, respectively. Lower seroprotection rates against Hib were observed in children who received 2 primary doses of DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib in 1 study (71.5%) and 2 primary doses of DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib in 2 studies (57.9% and 77.9%). Post-booster seroprotection or vaccine response rates were ≥88.9% against all antigens and were generally similar for both schedules.

### 3.3. Safety and reactogenicity profiles in head-to-head studies

#### 3.3.1. DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib versus DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib

While individual studies concluded that both vaccines have acceptable safety profiles, a recent meta-analysis of head-to-head trials showed that redness, pain, swelling, fever, persistent crying, irritability, drowsiness and anorexia were less frequent with DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib versus DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib. Grade 3 systemic reactions were less common and there was a trend towards lower frequency of grade 3 local reactions in DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib versus DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib vaccinees. The occurrence of serious adverse events (SAEs) ranged from <2.4% to 6.0% in any vaccination group.

## 4. Discussion

This critical review of head-to-head studies comparing DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib or DT5aP-HBV-IPV-Hib with DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib summarizes the observed differences between currently available hexavalent vaccines. While the 2 more recent vaccines were registered based on studies demonstrating non-inferiority of their immunogenicity versus comparators, including DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib, equivalence was never demonstrated, no studies were powered to assess superiority of immune responses induced by a hexavalent vaccine versus another, and different non-inferiority thresholds were used (up to a 10% difference).

In head-to-head studies, several differences were observed in immune responses to D, T and poliovirus types 1, 2 and 3, but antibody concentrations were far above the well-defined seroprotection thresholds in all groups for these antigens. Differences were also observed between the 3 vaccines for pertussis, PRP and HBV antigens in terms of post-primary and/or postbooster antibody concentrations, but their clinical relevance was not established. Concerning long-term antibody persistence, safety and impact of vaccination, data are more limited for DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib and DT5aP-HBV-IPV-Hib, which were authorized in Europe in 2013 and 2016, than for DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib, which has been licensed for >20 years and for which clinical and post-marketing evaluations are more comprehensive.

For pertussis, immune responses to PT seemed lower, and to FHA higher in DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib versus DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib vaccinees. No head-to-head studies evaluated immune responses against PRN following DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib versus DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib vaccination since this antigen was absent from the investigational vaccine (DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib). The PRN protein promotes adherence of Bordetella pertussis to human epithelial cells. The inclusion of PRN in the vaccine leads to production of anti-PRN antibodies, which play a key role in the opsonophagocytosis of the bacteria. In DT5aP-HBV-IPV-Hib recipients, immune responses to PT seemed higher and to PRN and FHA lower versus DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib recipients. Anti-FHA antibody GMTs were close to pre-vaccination levels before the toddler dose administration in DT5aP-HBV-IPV-Hib vaccinees, suggesting a rapid waning of immunity. Of note, the presence of additional antigens in DT5aP-HBV-IPV-Hib (Fimbriae types 2 and 3 antigens) may offer supplementary protection against pertussis. While immunogenicity results are difficult to interpret for pertussis in the absence of correlates of protection, the duration of protection induced by hexavalent vaccines remains an important question. Persistence of anti-PT and anti-FHA antibodies was similar in 4.5 year-old children who had received 3 + 1 doses of DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib and DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib. In contrast, differences observed shortly after primary vaccination in DT5aP-HBV-IPV-Hib versus DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib recipients, namely higher anti-PT antibody GMCs and lower anti-FHA antibody GMCs in DT5aP-HBV-IPV-Hib vaccinees, seemed to be maintained up to 4 years after completion of the 2 + 1 schedule. While no studies evaluated the long-term impact beyond 4 to 5 years of the 2 most recent hexavalent vaccines, routine infant vaccination with DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib had a beneficial impact on pertussis incidence during 10 years in Australia. Retrospective studies showed that in Italy, where DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib has been almost exclusively used since 2001 and vaccination coverage increased from 83.4% in 2002 to >95% by 2011, pertussis-related hospitalization rates progressively decreased from 1.23 to 0.64 per 100,000 inhabitants between 2001 and 2014. Vaccine effectiveness of DT3aP combination vaccines (both pentavalent and hexavalent vaccines) against pertussis hospitalizations was 100% (95% CI: 97.9%–100%) following a 3 + 1 vaccination course in infants in a nationwide study in Switzerland. Effectiveness against pertussis of DT3aP (2 + 1 schedule) was also observed during approximately 10 years in postmarketing studies in Sweden, where DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib has been almost exclusively used.

For Hib, immune responses post-primary vaccination were lower with DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib compared with the other hexavalent vaccines. After the toddler dose, anti-PRP antibody concentrations seemed lower in DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib versus DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib recipients, but the opposite was observed in DT5aP-HBV-IPV-Hib vaccinees. This could be explained by differential mechanisms related to the respective carrier proteins. Although no trials evaluated proportions of children with anti-PRP antibody concentrations ≥5 µg/ml, which are potentially correlated to the prevention of colonization, post-booster anti-PRP antibody GMCs were systematically above this threshold for DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib and DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib. While persistence of anti-PRP antibodies was similar in 4.5-year-old children who received 3 + 1 doses of DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib and DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib, no studies compared the persistence of anti-PRP antibodies in DT5aP-HBV-IPV-Hib versus DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib recipients. The estimated effectiveness of hexavalent vaccines (DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib and Hexavac) against Hib was 90.4% after 3-dose primary vaccination and 100% post-toddler dose in a nationwide surveillance German study. In a case-control study in the Netherlands, vaccine effectiveness of DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib against Hib was 94.0% (95% CI: 89.0%–96.8%), but waned with age. After hexavalent vaccine implementation into immunization programs in Italy, Hib-related hospitalization rates declined from 5.4 to 2.4 per 100,000 population among infants and from 2.3 to 0.9 per 100,000 population among 1–4-year-old children between 2001 and 2005. In Europe, the impact of Hib vaccination was sustained between 2006 and 2013, when DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib was almost exclusively used. Further studies are needed to evaluate vaccine efficacy/effectiveness of the 2 more recent hexavalent vaccines against Hib.

Another difference between vaccines is the HBV component (derived from H. polymorpha for DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib and from S. cerevisiae for the 2 other vaccines). While in some studies lower anti-HBV antibody concentrations were induced by DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib and DT5aP-HBV-IPV-Hib versus DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib, no differences were detected in others. Discrepancies in anti-HBV immune responses measured in DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib vaccinees may partly be explained by differences in anti-HBV assays used across head-to-head trials. Indeed, the tests used in comparative studies are always directed against the antigens of the investigational vaccine (DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib or DT5aP-HBV-IPV-Hib in our review). Long-term anti-HBV antibody persistence was similar in 9- to 10-year-old children who received 3 primary doses of DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib versus DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib in Thailand, and strong anamnestic responses were observed in both groups. Anti-HBV antibody GMCs were also similar in 4.5-year-old children who received 3 + 1 doses of DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib and DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib in Colombia. In another study conducted in Finland, anti-HBV antibody GMCs were slightly lower for 6-year-old children who had received 2 + 1 doses of DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib versus DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib, but strong anamnestic responses to HBV challenges were observed for both groups (≥96.7%). For DT5aP-HBV-IPV-Hib, proportions of 4- to 5-year-old children with anti-HBV antibody concentrations ≥10 mIU/mL were significantly lower compared with DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib after 3 + 1 and 2 + 1 schedules, indicating a possible more rapid waning of immunity. Long-term anti-HBV antibody persistence following DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib vaccination has also been evaluated in 14–15-year-old adolescents after a 3 + 1 schedule in Germany and in 10–11-year-old children after a 2 + 1 schedule in Slovakia. In both studies, high anamnestic responses were observed following administration of an HBV challenge and the induction of anti-HBV antibody GMCs ≥10 mIU/mL seemed an indicator of long-term protection. Of note, a study of the monovalent HBV vaccine containing the same HBV component as DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib showed antibody persistence for ≥20 years post-immunization. For DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib (3 + 1 schedule following a birth dose of HBV vaccine), a long-term antibody persistence study in South Africa and Latin America showed that 92% of 4.5-year-old children had anti-HBV antibody concentrations ≥10 mIU/mL. The impact of hexavalent vaccine implementation on disease incidence was shown for DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib (2, 4 and 6 months of age) in Australia, where the overall incidence of HBV decreased from 33.1 to 24.2 cases per 100,000 population between 2009 and 2018. Additional studies are needed to evaluate the effectiveness and long-term impact on HBV incidence of the more recent vaccines.

Concerning vaccination schedules, 2 + 1 immunization programs are increasingly used in Europe following the success of their implementation in Sweden and Italy for the last 20 years. The 2 + 1 schedules decrease the cost and complexity of immunization programs but increase the importance of compliance and timeliness of vaccination and should only be considered in countries with very high vaccination coverage. Slightly lower immune responses towards most antigens were observed following administration of 2 versus 3 primary doses for the 3 hexavalent vaccines, but between studies comparisons should be interpreted cautiously, and the clinical relevance of these differences, in particular for Hib, has not been established. After the toddler dose, similar immune responses were observed for both schedules. For DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib, post-marketing data from Sweden and Italy showed effectiveness of the 2 + 1 schedule against pertussis, and a clinical trial in Slovakia showed that this schedule induced immune memory against HBV during ≥10 years. The immunogenicity and safety of the 2 + 1 schedule for DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib and DT5aP-HBV-IPV-Hib were evaluated in clinical trials, but additional effectiveness studies would be advisable to evaluate their impact on disease incidence and to ensure a successful long-term disease control.

From a public health perspective as well as for vaccinators, vaccinated children and their parents, safety is a priority and licensed vaccines must have a positive benefit/risk profile. While this was demonstrated for the 3 hexavalent vaccines, a recent meta-analysis of head-to-head trials showed a higher reactogenicity for DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib versus DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib. In this meta-analysis, the risk of experiencing 9 out of 11 analyzed solicited systemic and local adverse reactions was higher with DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib versus DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib, even though individual studies concluded that both vaccines have acceptable safety profiles. Additional studies are needed to be able to perform meta-analyses comparing safety outcomes of DT5aP-HBV-IPV-Hib versus DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib or DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib and to conclude that the safety profiles of DT5aP-HBV-IPV-Hib and DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib are similar.

This is the first review to summarize comparative immunogenicity and safety data from head-to-head studies for the 3 currently licensed hexavalent vaccines. The limitations include the absence of studies comparing the 3 vaccines altogether, the different vaccines co-administered in some studies, and the different assays used to measure immune responses to some antigens. A further limitation was the different times since launch, which is not allowing the same level of evidence for the 3 vaccines. Since no systematic search was performed to identify studies evaluating the efficacy, effectiveness or impact on diseases of hexavalent vaccines, some papers may have been missed and no comparisons could be made between vaccines for these endpoints.

## 5. Conclusion

Although the 3 licensed hexavalent vaccines are generally considered similar, this critical review of immunogenicity and safety data from head-to-head trials highlights that there are some differences between them, which could be related to differences in composition and formulation. The clinical relevance of the observed differences in immune responses between vaccines has not been established. Moreover, the demonstrated non-inferiority of the immunogenicity of the more recent vaccines versus DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib does not allow a full bridging to similar efficacy, effectiveness and safety. While the availability of DT3aP-HBV-IPV/Hib over 20 years allowed to collect a wealth of data on long-term safety, immune responses, effectiveness and impact on diseases in clinical and post-marketing studies and makes it a key pillar of pediatric immunization, data only begin to be available for DT2aP-HBV-IPV-Hib, and it is currently too early to have these data for DT5aP-HBV-IPV-Hib.
