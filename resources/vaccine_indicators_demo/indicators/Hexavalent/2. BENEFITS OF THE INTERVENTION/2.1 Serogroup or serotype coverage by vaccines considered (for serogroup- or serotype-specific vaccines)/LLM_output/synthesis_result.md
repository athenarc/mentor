Hexavalent (DTaP–IPV–HB–Hib) vaccines are multi-pathogen combination products; among their components, serogroup/serotype-specific coverage is relevant primarily for *Haemophilus influenzae* (Hib) and poliovirus [3, 4].

- **Haemophilus influenzae (Hib component)**
  - Encapsulated *H. influenzae* comprises six capsular serotypes (a–f), while non-typeable strains lack a capsule [1, 5].  
  - The Hib component in hexavalent vaccines is serotype-specific: it targets *H. influenzae* **type b** capsular polysaccharide (polyribosylribitol phosphate; PRP), typically as a conjugate (e.g., PRP~T) [3, 4, 5].  
  - Evidence notes the theoretical concern that vaccines targeting a single serotype could select for capsule switching/replacement with non-vaccine capsular types or for loss of capsule (non-typeable strains) [1].  
  - Contextually, serotype b has been reported to account for approximately 95% of invasive *H. influenzae* disease serotypes [5].

- **Poliovirus (IPV component)**
  - Poliomyelitis is caused by three poliovirus serotypes (types 1, 2, and 3) [6].  
  - The IPV component used in hexavalent vaccines is **trivalent**, providing serotype coverage against poliovirus **types 1, 2, and 3** [3, 6]. Product-specific strain designations for IPV in one hexavalent vaccine (Hexaxim®) are: type 1 (Mahoney), type 2 (MEF-1), and type 3 (Saukett) [3].

- **Diphtheria, tetanus, pertussis, hepatitis B**
  - The extracted evidence describes these as included disease targets of hexavalent vaccines but does not provide serogroup/serotype coverage details for these components [3, 4].
