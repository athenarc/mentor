# Systematic Review Report

## 1. Review Summary
- **Title:** Cotrimoxazole prophylaxis for opportunistic infections in children with HIV infection
- **Objective:** To assess the effects of routinely administered cotrimoxazole on death and illness episodes in children with HIV infection, and in infants of HIV‐infected mothers.

## 2. Search Results
- Number of candidate studies found: 10

## 3. Screening Results
- Included studies: 1
- Excluded studies: 9
- Decisions:
  - Study 1: INCLUDE — Double-blind randomised placebo-controlled trial in children aged 1-14 years with HIV infection. Intervention is daily cotrimoxazole prophylaxis vs placebo. Fully meets all criteria: RCT, correct population (children <15 with HIV), routine prophylaxis, placebo comparator.
  - Study 2: EXCLUDE — Randomised trial but population is HIV-infected adults, not children <15 years or infants <18 months with HIV-infected mothers. Wrong population.
  - Study 3: EXCLUDE — Randomised trial of cotrimoxazole prophylaxis, but population is adults (patients with Kaposi's sarcoma, typical adult AIDS presentation; adult dosing 800/160 mg twice daily; no mention of children). Does not meet age criteria. Wrong population.
  - Study 4: EXCLUDE — Case series of four homosexual men; not a randomised or quasi-randomised trial. Also wrong population (adults). Not an intervention study.
  - Study 5: EXCLUDE — Cross-sectional necropsy study of children who died; no intervention, not a trial. Does not compare cotrimoxazole vs placebo/no treatment.
  - Study 6: EXCLUDE — Prospective cohort study (observational) comparing HIV-infected vs uninfected children. Not a randomised or quasi-randomised trial. No intervention of cotrimoxazole prophylaxis.
  - Study 7: EXCLUDE — Prospective observational study of morbidity in HIV-infected and uninfected children. Not a trial; no cotrimoxazole prophylaxis intervention.
  - Study 8: EXCLUDE — Randomised controlled trial but in HIV-infected adults with tuberculosis (median age 32, range 18-64). Does not meet population criteria (children <15 or infants <18 months).
  - Study 9: EXCLUDE — Review/editorial about mother-to-child transmission. Not a trial, no intervention. Not relevant study design.
  - Study 10: EXCLUDE — Prospective observational study of PCP incidence in HIV-infected children. Not a randomised or quasi-randomised trial. Although some children were on prophylaxis, the study does not compare cotrimoxazole vs placebo/no treatment in a controlled design.

## 4. Evidence Extraction Summary
**Study 1** — CHAP trial 2004
- **Population:** Children aged 1–14 years with clinical features of HIV infection; 7 later found HIV-negative and excluded from analysis.
- **Setting:** Zambia
- **Intervention:** Daily cotrimoxazole (trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole) prophylaxis; dose and start timing not specified in abstract.
- **Comparator:** Placebo (double-blind); standard care not specified.
- **Outcomes:**
  - All-cause mortality (overall): HR 0.57 (95% CI 0.43–0.77); p=0.0002
  - All-cause mortality (>12 months subgroup): HR 0.48 (95% CI 0.27–0.84)
  - Grade 3/4 adverse events (any): RR 0.86
  - Severe neutropenia (<0.5×10^9/L at least once): RR 2.0; p=0.06
  - Rash (any grade)
  - PCP detection among pneumonia NPA samples
- **Adverse events:**
  - Grade 3/4 AEs: 16 (6%) vs 18 (7%).
  - Severe neutropenia (<0.5×10^9/L once): 16 (6%) vs 7 (3%), p=0.06.
  - Rash: one case (placebo arm).
- **Study design:** RCT
- **Follow-up:** 19 months (median)
- **Key findings:**
  - Deaths: 74 (28%) cotrimoxazole vs 112 (42%) placebo.
  - All-cause mortality HR 0.57 (95% CI 0.43-0.77), p=0.0002.
  - >12 months follow-up subgroup: HR 0.48 (95% CI 0.27-0.84).
  - No heterogeneity of effect by age (test for heterogeneity p=0.82).
  - No heterogeneity of effect by baseline CD4 (test for heterogeneity p=0.36).
- **Notes:** Randomized: 541. Post-randomization exclusions: 7 HIV-negative (abstract states ITT). Approximate arm sizes (from %): cotrimoxazole ≈ 264, placebo ≈ 267 (exact Ns not reported in abstract). Design: double-blind, placebo-controlled, individually randomized (RCT); analysis by intention-to-treat; DSMC recommended early stopping. ART availability/use: not reported in abstract. Local context: area with high levels of bacterial resistance to cotrimoxazole (per abstract). Pneumocystis identified by immunofluorescence in 1/73 NPA from pneumonia cases (placebo arm).


## 5. Synthesis & Conclusions
Study characteristics: One double-blind, individually randomized, placebo-controlled trial from Zambia evaluated daily cotrimoxazole prophylaxis in children aged 1–14 years with clinical features of HIV infection over a median 19 months of follow-up [1]. Seven children later found HIV-negative were excluded; an intention-to-treat approach was stated. The regimen was daily cotrimoxazole; dosing and start timing were not specified in the abstract. The comparator was placebo.

All-cause mortality: Cotrimoxazole significantly reduced mortality versus placebo (HR 0.57, 95% CI 0.43–0.77; p=0.0002) [1]. The benefit appeared sustained beyond 12 months (HR 0.48, 95% CI 0.27–0.84) [1]. Within-study subgroup analyses found no heterogeneity by age or baseline CD4 count [1]. Precision for the primary effect was good, with confidence intervals clearly excluding no effect.

Illness episodes (PCP and serious bacterial infections): The abstract did not provide comparative counts for pneumonia or serious bacterial infections. Among pneumonia cases sampled, Pneumocystis jiroveci was identified in 1/73 nasopharyngeal aspirates from the placebo arm [1], suggesting low detection in tested samples, but this does not permit comparative efficacy estimates for PCP or serious bacterial infections.

Hospitalizations: Hospitalization outcomes were not reported in the abstract, precluding conclusions about impacts on inpatient care.

Adverse events: Overall grade 3/4 adverse events were similar between groups (6% vs 7%) [1]. Severe neutropenia (<0.5×10^9/L at least once) occurred more often in the cotrimoxazole arm (6% vs 3%), with borderline statistical significance (p=0.06) [1]. Rash was rare and reported once in the placebo arm [1]. These data suggest acceptable safety with possible increased risk of severe neutropenia requiring monitoring.

Heterogeneity and context: Only one trial was identified, so between-study heterogeneity cannot be assessed. Within the trial, effects were consistent across age and CD4 strata [1]. The study occurred in a setting with high background bacterial resistance to cotrimoxazole, yet a mortality benefit persisted [1]. ART availability/use was not reported and dosing details were not provided; given the 2004 era and setting, the context likely reflects limited ART access compared with current standards. Follow-up was a median of 19 months [1]. No trials of cotrimoxazole prophylaxis in HIV-exposed but uninfected infants were included in the extracted evidence, so effects in that population remain uncertain here.

Consistency and precision: Findings for mortality were precise and internally consistent within [1]. Morbidity, hospitalization, and resistance outcomes were insufficiently reported to assess consistency or precision across these domains.

Antimicrobial resistance: No direct resistance outcomes (e.g., carriage or infection with resistant organisms) were reported. The observed mortality benefit despite high background resistance suggests that prophylaxis may confer net clinical benefit through reductions in severe bacterial infections and other opportunistic processes, but dedicated resistance monitoring was not described in [1].

Implications for practice in low-resource settings: In HIV-infected children, routine daily cotrimoxazole prophylaxis reduces all-cause mortality with acceptable safety in a low-resource, high-resistance setting [1]. Programs should consider continued prophylaxis—especially where ART initiations are delayed or immune reconstitution is incomplete—while monitoring for hematologic toxicity (e.g., neutropenia). Given the absence of evidence here for HIV-exposed but uninfected infants, practice in that group should be guided by broader policy and contemporary evidence not contained in this extraction.

Research gaps: Modern ART-era evaluations are needed to define the incremental benefit of cotrimoxazole alongside early and effective ART, optimal start/stop criteria (e.g., by age, CD4 recovery, or virologic suppression), effects on hospitalizations and specific serious bacterial infections, comprehensive safety (including hematologic monitoring), and surveillance for antimicrobial resistance. Clear reporting of dosing regimens and co-interventions is needed.

## 6. Quality Assessment
- **Study 1:** Quality rating — moderate
  - Strength: Individually randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled design
  - Strength: Large sample (n=541) with intention-to-treat analysis stated
  - Strength: Clinically important, objective primary outcome (all-cause mortality)
  - Strength: Effect consistent across age and baseline CD4 subgroups
  - Strength: Conducted in a high background bacterial resistance setting, yet mortality benefit observed
  - Limitation: Data Safety Monitoring Committee recommended early stopping, which can overestimate treatment effects
  - Limitation: Allocation concealment not reported in abstract
  - Limitation: Attrition and adherence details not fully described
  - Limitation: Seven post-randomization exclusions (HIV-negative) noted
  - Limitation: Limited reporting on specific illness episodes (PCP, serious bacterial infections), hospitalizations, and dosing regimen
  - Limitation: ART availability/use not reported (likely pre-ART or early-ART context), limiting applicability to current ART-era settings
  - Bias concern: Potential bias from early stopping for benefit
  - Bias concern: Unclear allocation concealment
  - Bias concern: Incomplete reporting of attrition and adherence
  - Bias concern: Selective outcome reporting possible for morbidity/hospitalization endpoints
  - Applicability: direct

**Overall evidence strength:** moderate

**Key limitations:**
- Only one RCT from a single country; no replication across diverse settings
- Insufficient reporting on morbidity (PCP, serious bacterial infections) and hospitalizations
- Dosing regimen and ART co-interventions not described in the abstract
- Early stopping may overestimate mortality benefit
- No direct antimicrobial resistance outcomes reported
- No evidence included for HIV-exposed but uninfected infants
- Context likely pre-ART or limited ART availability, limiting applicability to current ART-era programs
