# Systematic Review Report

## 1. Review Summary
- **Title:** Cotrimoxazole prophylaxis for opportunistic infections in children with HIV infection
- **Objective:** To assess the effects of routinely administered cotrimoxazole on death and illness episodes in children with HIV infection, and in infants of HIV‐infected mothers.

## 2. Search Results
- Number of candidate studies found: 16

## 3. Screening Results
- Included studies: 9
- Excluded studies: 7
- Decisions:
  - Study 1: EXCLUDE — Narrative overview; mentions an ongoing CTX trial in malnourished children but not a completed randomized CTX vs placebo/no-treatment trial in HIV-infected or HIV-exposed infants.
  - Study 2: INCLUDE — Participants were from CHAP, a randomized trial comparing CTX vs placebo in HIV-infected children aged 1–14 years; meets design and population criteria, despite focus on prognostic analysis.
  - Study 3: INCLUDE — Randomized, placebo-controlled CHAP trial in HIV-infected children comparing CTX vs placebo with mortality and hospitalization outcomes.
  - Study 4: INCLUDE — Includes randomized CTX vs placebo phase of CHAP in HIV-infected children; although post-trial observational data are also presented, the trial phase meets criteria.
  - Study 5: INCLUDE — Substudy of a randomized CTX vs placebo trial (CHAP) in HIV-infected children; randomized comparison present in target population.
  - Study 6: INCLUDE — Randomized CTX vs placebo (CHAP) among HIV-infected children with growth and hemoglobin outcomes.
  - Study 7: EXCLUDE — Commentary/perspective; not a randomized or quasi-randomized CTX vs placebo/no-treatment trial.
  - Study 8: INCLUDE — Secondary analysis from a randomized CTX vs placebo trial (CHAP) in HIV-infected children; randomized comparison context in target population.

## 4. Evidence Extraction Summary
**Study 2** — Determinants of Survival Without Antiretroviral Therapy After Infancy in HIV-1-Infected Zambian Children in the CHAP Trial
- **Population:** HIV-1–infected Zambian children aged 1–14 years (median 5.5); ART-naive; n=514; 67% weight-for-age <3rd percentile at baseline; median CD4% = 11%.
- **Setting:** Zambia; participants from the CHAP randomized trial context.
- **Intervention:** Within CHAP trial context: once-daily cotrimoxazole prophylaxis (dose not specified in abstract).
- **Comparator:** Placebo (in parent CHAP RCT); this analysis primarily assessed prognostic factors irrespective of randomization arm.
- **Outcomes:**
  - All-cause mortality
- **Adverse events:** Not reported in abstract.
- **Study design:** Prognostic cohort analysis within RCT population (Cox models).
- **Follow-up:** 607 child-years total; maximum 2.6 years.
- **Key findings:**
  - 165 deaths occurred: 27.2 per 100 child-years (95% CI 23.3–31.6).
  - Independent predictors of poorer survival: low weight-for-age, low CD4%, low hemoglobin, maternal primary caregiver, current malnutrition, prior hospitalizations for respiratory or recurrent severe bacterial infections.
  - Oral candidiasis predicted poorer survival only when baseline CD4% not considered.
  - WHO clinical stage (retrospectively applied) mortality per 100 child-years: Stage 4 = 44.5 (37.2–53.2), Stage 3 = 14.7 (10.9–19.8), Stage 1/2 = 2.3 (0.3–16.7); very low weight-for-age was the only staging feature for 42% of Stage 4 children.
- **Notes:** ART not available during most of this period; analysis focused on prognostic factors rather than direct CTX vs placebo effect.

**Study 3** — Effect of cotrimoxazole on causes of death, hospital admissions and antibiotic use in HIV-infected children
- **Population:** HIV-1–infected Zambian children; n=534; median age 4.4 years (32% aged 1–2 years); ART-naive.
- **Setting:** Zambia; CHAP randomized placebo-controlled trial; background high bacterial resistance to cotrimoxazole.
- **Intervention:** Once-daily cotrimoxazole prophylaxis (dose not specified in abstract).
- **Comparator:** Placebo.
- **Outcomes:**
  - All-cause mortality (overall)
  - Hospital admissions for serious bacterial infections (rate): RR 0.6701754385964913; p=0.09
  - Death in hospital from serious bacterial infection (2-year cumulative probability); p=0.08
- **Adverse events:** Not reported in abstract.
- **Study design:** Randomized, placebo-controlled trial (secondary analyses of outcomes).
- **Follow-up:** Up to 2 years cumulative probability estimates reported; trial period 2001–2003.
- **Key findings:**
  - 186 deaths overall; 166 children had ≥1 hospital admission not ending in death.
  - Cotrimoxazole was associated with lower mortality both outside hospital (P=0.01) and following hospital admission (P=0.005).
  - Largest excess of hospital deaths in placebo group due to respiratory infections: 22/56 (39%) placebo vs 10/35 (29%) cotrimoxazole.
  - 2-year cumulative probability of dying in hospital from serious bacterial infection: 7% on cotrimoxazole vs 12% on placebo (P=0.08).
  - Admission rates for serious bacterial infections: 19.1 vs 28.5 per 100 child-years (cotrimoxazole vs placebo), P=0.09; estimated rate ratio ≈ 0.67.
  - Despite less total follow-up due to higher mortality, placebo group received more antibiotic days (especially penicillin) in year one: 6083 vs 4972 days, P=0.05.
- **Notes:** Benefits appeared concentrated on respiratory infections; implications for continuing CTX after ART initiation noted by authors.

**Study 4** — The Impact of Daily Cotrimoxazole Prophylaxis and Antiretroviral Therapy on Mortality and Hospital Admissions in HIV-Infected Zambian Children
- **Population:** HIV-infected Zambian children from CHAP; n=534 during trial; post-trial closed cohort follow-up.
- **Setting:** Zambia; CHAP trial (2001–2003) with post-trial observation to June 2006; ART became available from Aug 2004.
- **Intervention:** Once-daily cotrimoxazole prophylaxis during the trial; post-trial, placebo group initiated cotrimoxazole.
- **Comparator:** Placebo during the trial phase; post-trial both groups on cotrimoxazole; ART availability periods analyzed.
- **Outcomes:**
  - All-cause mortality (trial period rate cotrimoxazole vs placebo): RR 0.6086956521739131
  - Hospital admissions (trial period rate cotrimoxazole vs placebo): RR 0.6857142857142857
  - All-cause mortality (post-ART availability periods combined groups)
  - Hospital admissions (post-ART availability periods combined groups)
- **Adverse events:** Not reported in abstract.
- **Study design:** Randomized, placebo-controlled trial with post-trial closed cohort analysis over calendar time.
- **Follow-up:** Post-trial: 546 child-years; 40 deaths; 80 hospital admissions through June 2006.
- **Key findings:**
  - During the trial: mortality 14 (95% CI 9–21) vs 23 (16–34) deaths per 100 child-years (cotrimoxazole vs placebo); hospital admissions 24 (15–39) vs 35 (23–53) per 100 child-years.
  - After trial closure, rates remained stable in the original cotrimoxazole group but decreased to 15 (8–26) deaths and 19 (10–41) admissions per 100 child-years in the original placebo group after initiating cotrimoxazole.
  - With ART availability: combined-group mortality declined to 6 (95% CI 3–11) and then 2 (0.8–6) deaths per 100 child-years; hospital admissions to 17 (11–27) and 8 (4–15) per 100 child-years.
  - Estimated trial-period rate ratios: mortality ≈ 0.61; hospital admissions ≈ 0.69 (approximated from reported rates).
- **Notes:** ART introduction associated with ~6-fold mortality and ~3-fold hospitalization reductions; cotrimoxazole benefits persisted through trial and post-trial.

**Study 5** — Impact of Cotrimoxazole on Carriage and Antibiotic Resistance of Streptococcus pneumoniae and Haemophilus influenzae in HIV-Infected Children in Zambia
- **Population:** Substudy of CHAP: HIV-infected Zambian children; n=181 with baseline NP swabs (92 CTX, 89 placebo).
- **Setting:** Zambia; nasopharyngeal carriage/resistance in CHAP participants.
- **Intervention:** Cotrimoxazole prophylaxis (daily; dose not specified).
- **Comparator:** Placebo.
- **Outcomes:**
  - Antimicrobial resistance in S. pneumoniae (cotrimoxazole); p=0.0001
  - Antimicrobial resistance in H. influenzae (cotrimoxazole); p=0.005
  - Carriage prevalence changes (S. pneumoniae, H. influenzae)
- **Adverse events:** Not reported in abstract.
- **Study design:** Randomized, placebo-controlled trial substudy.
- **Follow-up:** Median time from randomization to last specimen: 56 months (CTX) vs 48 months (placebo), P=0.001.
- **Key findings:**
  - Cotrimoxazole increased post-baseline CTX resistance in S. pneumoniae (P<0.0001) and H. influenzae (P=0.005).
  - In S. pneumoniae, CTX decreased switching from resistance to sensitivity (P=0.002) and suggested lower probability of remaining carriage-free (P=0.10).
  - In H. influenzae, CTX decreased switching from carriage to no carriage (P=0.02) and reduced chance of remaining CTX sensitive (P=0.05).
  - Carriage of H. influenzae unaltered overall by CTX; small non-significant increase in S. pneumoniae carriage in placebo arm.
  - No associations of carriage with CD4%, change in CD4%, age, gender, or sampling month.
- **Notes:** Background high CTX resistance; findings indicate selection for CTX resistance among commensals/pathogens with prophylaxis.

**Study 6** — Improved Growth and Anemia in HIV-Infected African Children Taking Cotrimoxazole Prophylaxis
- **Population:** HIV-infected, ART-naive Zambian children; n=541; enrolled in CHAP.
- **Setting:** Zambia; CHAP randomized placebo-controlled trial.
- **Intervention:** Cotrimoxazole prophylaxis (daily; dose not specified).
- **Comparator:** Placebo.
- **Outcomes:**
  - Growth: change in weight-for-age z-score (slope); p=0.04
  - Growth: change in height-for-age z-score (slope); p=0.01
  - Hemoglobin: change over time; p=0.01
- **Adverse events:** Not reported in abstract.
- **Study design:** Randomized, placebo-controlled trial (secondary growth/hematologic analysis).
- **Follow-up:** Trial follow-up duration as per CHAP (up to ~2 years).
- **Key findings:**
  - Children on cotrimoxazole had slower declines in weight-for-age (P=0.04) and height-for-age (P=0.01).
  - Greater increase in hemoglobin in cotrimoxazole group (P=0.01).
  - Findings support expanded early CTX use in HIV-infected children.
- **Notes:** Improvements observed despite high background bacterial resistance; ART-naive cohort.

**Study 8** — Adherence to Both Cotrimoxazole and Placebo is Associated with Improved Survival Among HIV-Infected Zambian Children
- **Population:** HIV-infected Zambian children in CHAP surviving >28 days; n=496; 153 deaths during follow-up.
- **Setting:** Zambia; CHAP RCT adherence substudy.
- **Intervention:** Cotrimoxazole prophylaxis (daily) vs placebo; adherence assessed by bottle weights and caregiver report.
- **Comparator:** Placebo; adherence metrics contrasted; mortality risk modeled vs adherence.
- **Outcomes:**
  - All-cause mortality risk per 10% lower bottle-weight adherence (combined CTX/placebo): HR 1.1
- **Adverse events:** Not reported in abstract.
- **Study design:** Secondary analysis using Cox regression within RCT.
- **Follow-up:** Adherence and subsequent mortality assessed over trial follow-up.
- **Key findings:**
  - Adherence high and similar by arm: bottle weights showed 100% adherence at 71% of visits; caregiver report 100% at 79% of visits.
  - Every 10% lower adherence (bottle weights) associated with 10–11% increased mortality risk (HR ≈ 1.10–1.11).
  - Associations persisted after adjustment for baseline survival predictors and caregiver changes.
  - Caregiver-reported adherence not associated with survival.
  - Association between adherence to placebo and survival likely reflects unmeasured caregiver effects.
- **Notes:** Findings highlight importance of accurate adherence measurement; implications for quantifying ART adherence effects.

**Study 11** — Prolonged Cotrimoxazole Prophylaxis Has No Impact on Child Growth in the First Two Years of Life: Findings from a Randomized Controlled Trial in Botswana
- **Population:** HIV-exposed uninfected infants; n=2848 enrolled; Botswana Mpepu randomized placebo-controlled trial.
- **Setting:** Botswana; infants followed through 18 months.
- **Intervention:** Prolonged cotrimoxazole prophylaxis from 14–34 days of life through 15 months (dose schedule not specified in abstract).
- **Comparator:** Placebo.
- **Outcomes:**
  - Growth: mean weight-for-age z-score through 18 months
  - Growth: mean length-for-age z-score through 18 months
  - Growth: mean weight-for-length z-score through 18 months
- **Adverse events:** Not reported in abstract.
- **Study design:** Randomized, placebo-controlled trial.
- **Follow-up:** Through 18 months of age.
- **Key findings:**
  - No significant differences in mean WAZ, LAZ, or WLZ between cotrimoxazole and placebo through 18 months.
- **Notes:** HEU population; growth-neutral effect in this setting.

**Study 13** — Efficacy and safety of three regimens for the prevention of malaria in young HIV-exposed Ugandan children
- **Population:** HIV-exposed uninfected children; 200 enrolled at 4–5 months; 186 randomized after cessation of breastfeeding (median 10 months).
- **Setting:** Tororo, Uganda; rural area with intense, year-round malaria transmission.
- **Intervention:** Daily trimethoprim–sulfamethoxazole (TMP-SMX) from randomization to 24 months.
- **Comparator:** No chemoprevention (also evaluated monthly SP and monthly dihydroartemisinin–piperaquine arms).
- **Outcomes:**
  - Malaria incidence (TMP-SMX vs no chemoprevention): RR 0.51 (95% CI 0.34–0.77); p=0.001
  - Hospitalization incidence
  - Diarrhoeal illness incidence
  - Respiratory tract infection incidence
  - Anaemia prevalence
  - Asymptomatic parasitaemia prevalence (DP arm lower; TMP-SMX not specified)
- **Adverse events:** Monthly DP was well tolerated; no significant safety differences reported among arms (per abstract).
- **Study design:** Open-label, randomized controlled trial.
- **Follow-up:** Intervention from randomization to 24 months; additional 1-year post-intervention follow-up for malaria incidence.
- **Key findings:**
  - Malaria incidence in no-chemoprevention group: 6.28 episodes per person-year at risk.
  - Protective efficacy vs no chemoprevention: DP 69% (95% CI 53–80, P<0.001), TMP-SMX 49% (95% CI 23–66, P=0.001), SP 9% (95% CI −35 to 38, P=0.65).
  - Converted RR for TMP-SMX vs no chemoprevention ≈ 0.51 (95% CI 0.34–0.77).
  - No significant differences in secondary outcomes except lower asymptomatic parasitaemia prevalence with DP.
- **Notes:** HEU setting; malaria-endemic area; study supports extended TMP-SMX prophylaxis benefits against malaria.

**Study 14** — Cotrimoxazole prophylaxis was associated with enteric commensal bacterial resistance among HIV‐exposed infants in a randomized controlled trial, Botswana
- **Population:** Subset of HIV-exposed uninfected infants in Botswana Mpepu trial; stool samples from 220 infants (381 samples: 118 at randomization, 151 at 3 months, 112 at 6 months).
- **Setting:** Botswana; randomized, placebo-controlled infant CTX prophylaxis trial.
- **Intervention:** Prophylactic cotrimoxazole from 14–34 days of life through 15 months (dose not specified).
- **Comparator:** Placebo.
- **Outcomes:**
  - CTX resistance prevalence in commensal E. coli at 3 and 6 months; p=0.01
  - CTX resistance prevalence in commensal Klebsiella spp. at 3 and 6 months; p=0.01
- **Adverse events:** Not reported in abstract.
- **Study design:** Randomized, placebo-controlled trial substudy (microbiology).
- **Follow-up:** Samples at randomization, 3 months, 6 months.
- **Key findings:**
  - Baseline CTX resistance common in both arms: E. coli 52.2%, Klebsiella spp. 37.7%; no arm differences at baseline.
  - E. coli CTX resistance: 94.9% (CTX) vs 51.4% (placebo) at 3 months (P=0.01); 84.2% vs 57.5% at 6 months (P=0.01).
  - Klebsiella CTX resistance: 79.0% (CTX) vs 19.1% (placebo) at 3 months (P<0.01); 68.8% vs 14.3% at 6 months (P<0.01).
  - Findings indicate increased selection for CTX-resistant commensal bacteria with infant CTX prophylaxis.
- **Notes:** Assessed by Kirby-Bauer method; public health implications for antimicrobial resistance highlighted.


## 5. Synthesis & Conclusions
Primary outcomes — mortality and serious infections:
- HIV-infected children (CHAP, Zambia): Two randomized, placebo-controlled publications from the CHAP trial consistently show that daily cotrimoxazole reduces mortality and serious infection–related morbidity compared with placebo. During the blinded trial phase, estimated rate ratios favored cotrimoxazole for all-cause mortality (RR≈0.61) and hospital admissions (RR≈0.69) [4]; admissions for serious bacterial infections were lower (19.1 vs 28.5 per 100 child-years; RR≈0.67; P=0.09) and in-hospital death from serious bacterial infection was numerically reduced (7% vs 12% at 2 years; P=0.08) [3]. Additional analyses reported significantly lower mortality outside hospital (P=0.01) and following hospital admission (P=0.005) with cotrimoxazole [3]. After the trial ended, initiation of cotrimoxazole in prior placebo recipients was associated with declines in deaths and admissions, and the later availability of ART was associated with further large reductions in both outcomes [4]. Pneumocystis pneumonia (PCP)–specific events were not reported in these abstracts; benefits appeared concentrated in respiratory infections overall [3].

Secondary outcomes:
- Hospitalizations: Consistently reduced during the trial with cotrimoxazole vs placebo (RR≈0.69) [4]; serious bacterial infection admissions trend lower (RR≈0.67) [3].
- Malaria (HEU children in malaria-endemic Uganda): In an open-label RCT, daily trimethoprim–sulfamethoxazole reduced malaria incidence vs no chemoprevention (RR 0.51, 95% CI 0.34–0.77) [13]. Monthly dihydroartemisinin–piperaquine performed better, but TMP-SMX still conferred substantial protection [13].
- Growth and hematologic outcomes: Among HIV-infected children, cotrimoxazole was associated with slower declines in weight-for-age and height-for-age and greater hemoglobin increases [6]. In contrast, among HIV-exposed uninfected (HEU) infants in Botswana, prolonged prophylaxis to 15 months showed no effect on growth through 18 months [11].
- Antimicrobial resistance (AMR): In HIV-infected children in Zambia, cotrimoxazole prophylaxis increased resistance to cotrimoxazole among nasopharyngeal Streptococcus pneumoniae and Haemophilus influenzae and altered transition probabilities toward resistance [5]. In HEU infants in Botswana, cotrimoxazole prophylaxis markedly increased cotrimoxazole resistance among commensal E. coli and Klebsiella spp. at 3 and 6 months [14]. Across settings, CTX prophylaxis consistently selected for CTX-resistant commensals/pathogens.
- Adverse events: None of the included abstracts reported clinical adverse events in detail; safety signals were not highlighted in these summaries [3][4][5][6][11][13][14].

Heterogeneity and context:
- Population: Strong mortality and hospitalization benefits are demonstrated in HIV-infected, ART-naive African children [3][4], whereas benefits in HEU infants are context-dependent: growth-neutral in Botswana [11] but malaria-reducing in high-transmission Uganda [13].
- Setting and endemicity: Malaria protection with TMP-SMX is evident in a setting with intense transmission [13], which may not generalize to low-transmission areas.
- ART context: In CHAP, most children were ART-naive during the trial; later ART roll-out further reduced mortality and admissions [4]. This suggests cotrimoxazole benefits persist pre-ART and that combined ART+CTX may yield additive reductions, though post-trial observations are non-randomized.
- Antimicrobial resistance background: High baseline resistance was noted in Zambia and Botswana, yet clinical benefits on mortality/hospitalization persisted in HIV-infected children [3][4][6], underscoring that cotrimoxazole’s net clinical effect may extend beyond direct activity against fully susceptible organisms, potentially via prevention of some bacterial/respiratory infections.

Certainty and implications:
- Mortality/hospitalizations in HIV-infected children: Moderate-to-high certainty based on a large, blinded RCT program in one country with consistent effects and biologic plausibility [3][4].
- Malaria in HEU infants: Moderate certainty for protection in malaria-endemic settings from one open-label RCT [13].
- Growth outcomes: Low-to-moderate certainty—signals of improved growth/hemoglobin in HIV-infected children [6], but no growth effect in HEU infants [11]; these outcomes are secondary to the main clinical endpoints.
- AMR selection: Moderate certainty that CTX prophylaxis increases CTX resistance among commensal/pathogenic bacteria in both HIV-infected children and HEU infants [5][14]; clinical consequences were not quantified.
Overall, routinely administered cotrimoxazole reduces mortality and serious illness (notably respiratory infections) in HIV-infected African children and reduces malaria in HEU children in high-transmission settings, while selecting for CTX resistance in carriage flora. Adverse events were underreported in the included abstracts. Practice should continue to prioritize CTX in HIV-infected children (including with ART), with local malaria epidemiology guiding CTX use in HEU infants; surveillance for AMR and careful stewardship remain important. Evidence gaps include PCP-specific outcomes, adverse event quantification, multicountry trials in the ART era, and the clinical impact of AMR selection.

  - Limitation: Limited reporting of outcome ascertainment details

**Overall evidence strength:** moderate

**Key limitations:**
- Most efficacy evidence for mortality/hospitalizations derives from a single RCT program in one country (Zambia), potentially limiting generalizability.
- PCP-specific outcomes were not reported in the included abstracts, despite being a key rationale for prophylaxis.
- Adverse events and toxicity profiles were not reported, limiting safety assessment.
- Post-trial, ART-era analyses are observational and confounded by temporal trends.
- Malaria benefit shown in a single open-label trial in a high-transmission area; applicability to lower-transmission settings is uncertain.
- Antimicrobial resistance assessments show selection for CTX resistance but did not quantify clinical impact on invasive infections.
- Allocation concealment, blinding details, and selective reporting assessments were often not available from abstracts.
