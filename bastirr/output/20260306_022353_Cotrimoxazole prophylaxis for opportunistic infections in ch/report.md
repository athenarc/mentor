# Systematic Review Report

## 1. Review Summary
- **Title:** Cotrimoxazole prophylaxis for opportunistic infections in children with HIV infection
- **Objective:** To assess the effects of routinely administered cotrimoxazole on death and illness episodes in children with HIV infection, and in infants of HIV‐infected mothers.

## 2. Search Results
- Number of candidate studies found: 10

## 3. Screening Results
- Included studies: 1
- Excluded studies: 9
- Decisions:
  - Study 1: INCLUDE — Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial of routine cotrimoxazole prophylaxis in HIV-infected children aged 1–14 years; comparator is placebo; directly matches population, intervention, comparator, and design.
  - Study 2: EXCLUDE — Population is adults; not children <15 or HIV-exposed infants; does not meet the review population criteria.
  - Study 3: EXCLUDE — Randomized prophylaxis study but in adults with AIDS (Kaposi's sarcoma cohort); does not meet child or HIV-exposed infant population criteria.
  - Study 4: EXCLUDE — Case series in adult men; no randomized/quasi-randomized prophylaxis; wrong population and design.
  - Study 5: EXCLUDE — Observational necropsy study; no randomized prophylaxis intervention.
  - Study 6: EXCLUDE — Prospective cohort of HIV-infected and uninfected children; no randomized cotrimoxazole prophylaxis intervention.
  - Study 7: EXCLUDE — Prospective observational study; no randomized prophylaxis with cotrimoxazole versus placebo/no prophylaxis.
  - Study 8: EXCLUDE — Randomized trial but adult population (median age 32); does not meet child or HIV-exposed infant criteria.
  - Study 9: EXCLUDE — Narrative/review content; not a randomized or quasi-randomized prophylaxis trial.
  - Study 10: EXCLUDE — Prospective diagnostic/incidence study; although children and mentions prophylaxis status, there is no randomized assignment of cotrimoxazole versus placebo/no prophylaxis.

## 4. Evidence Extraction Summary
**Study 1** — Chintu et al. 2004 (likely; author/year not provided in abstract)
- **Population:** Children aged 1–14 years in Zambia with clinical features of HIV infection; 541 randomized; 7 later found HIV-negative and excluded (thus HIV-infected children analyzed).
- **Setting:** Zambia; double-blind randomized placebo-controlled trial conducted in a setting with high bacterial resistance to co-trimoxazole.
- **Intervention:** Daily co-trimoxazole (trimethoprim–sulfamethoxazole) prophylaxis; dosing/timing not specified in the abstract; administered throughout follow-up.
- **Comparator:** Placebo (daily), double-blind.
- **Outcomes:**
  - All-cause mortality (intention-to-treat): HR 0.57 (95% CI 0.43–0.77); p=0.0002
  - All-cause mortality among those followed >12 months: HR 0.48 (95% CI 0.27–0.84)
  - Grade 3/4 adverse events (any)
  - Severe neutropenia (<0.5×10^9/L at least once); p=0.06
  - Pneumocystis carinii (PCP) detection among pneumonia cases (NPA immunofluorescence)
- **Adverse events:**
  - Grade 3/4 adverse events: 16/≈? (6%) in co-trimoxazole vs 18/≈? (7%) in placebo.
  - Severe neutropenia on one occasion (<0.5×10^9/L): 16 (6%) co-trimoxazole vs 7 (3%) placebo; p=0.06.
  - Rash: 1 event reported in placebo group.
- **Study design:** RCT
- **Follow-up:** Median 19 months; subgroup analysis for those followed beyond 12 months (n=320).
- **Key findings:**
  - Mortality significantly reduced with daily co-trimoxazole vs placebo: deaths 74 (co-trimoxazole) vs 112 (placebo); HR 0.57 (95% CI 0.43–0.77), p=0.0002.
  - Benefit persisted among children followed >12 months: HR 0.48 (95% CI 0.27–0.84); test for heterogeneity by follow-up duration p=0.60.
  - Effect consistent across ages (heterogeneity p=0.82) and baseline CD4 counts (heterogeneity p=0.36).
  - No excess in overall grade 3/4 adverse events (6% vs 7%); trend toward more transient severe neutropenia with co-trimoxazole (6% vs 3%; p=0.06).
  - PCP (Pneumocystis carinii) rarely identified: 1/73 nasopharyngeal aspirates positive (placebo group).
- **Notes:** Trial was stopped early on DSMC recommendation (Oct 2003). Analysis by intention-to-treat. Area had high background bacterial resistance to co-trimoxazole, but resistance outcomes were not reported. Dosing schedule not provided in abstract. Numerators for deaths provided (74 vs 112) but per-arm denominators not specified in abstract.


## 5. Synthesis & Conclusions
[1] In HIV-infected Zambian children (1–14 years), daily cotrimoxazole prophylaxis reduced all-cause mortality versus placebo over a median 19-month follow-up (HR 0.57, 95% CI 0.43–0.77), with a sustained benefit among those followed beyond 12 months (HR 0.48, 95% CI 0.27–0.84). Effects were consistent across age and baseline CD4 strata. [1] Illness episodes: Pneumocystis pneumonia was rarely identified (1/73 NPAs, placebo arm), and the trial abstract did not report comparative rates for bacterial infections, malaria, or hospitalizations; thus morbidity effects beyond mortality remain uncertain. [1] Safety: Overall grade 3/4 adverse events were similar between arms (6% cotrimoxazole vs 7% placebo). There was a trend toward more transient severe neutropenia with cotrimoxazole (6% vs 3%; p=0.06), without an excess of other serious harms. Heterogeneity and applicability: Evidence derives from a single RCT in a low-resource, high–bacterial resistance setting (Zambia), using daily cotrimoxazole throughout follow-up. No data were available for HIV-exposed, uninfected infants. Outcome definitions for non-mortality morbidity were limited, and antimicrobial resistance emergence was not measured. Overall, the trial provides moderate-strength evidence that routine cotrimoxazole substantially reduces mortality in HIV-infected children in similar settings, with acceptable safety. Evidence is insufficient for effects on specific morbidity outcomes and for HIV-exposed infants.

## 6. Quality Assessment
- **Study 1:** Quality rating — moderate
  - Strength: Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled design
  - Strength: Intention-to-treat mortality analysis
  - Strength: Large effect size on all-cause mortality with narrow confidence interval
  - Strength: Effect consistent across age and baseline CD4 strata
  - Limitation: Trial stopped early by DSMC for benefit (potential to overestimate effect)
  - Limitation: Allocation concealment and sequence generation methods not explicitly reported in abstract
  - Limitation: Limited reporting on non-mortality clinical endpoints (bacterial infections, malaria, hospitalizations)
  - Limitation: Antimicrobial resistance outcomes not measured or reported
  - Limitation: Dose/regimen details not specified in abstract
  - Limitation: Single-country setting may limit generalizability
  - Bias concern: Unclear allocation concealment
  - Bias concern: Unclear sequence generation details
  - Bias concern: Early stopping for benefit (other bias)
  - Bias concern: Selective reporting possible for morbidity outcomes
  - Applicability: direct

**Overall evidence strength:** moderate

**Key limitations:**
- Single RCT from one country; external validity to other contexts uncertain
- Trial stopped early for benefit, which can overestimate treatment effects
- Limited reporting on non-mortality morbidity outcomes (bacterial infections, malaria, hospital admissions)
- No data for HIV-exposed, uninfected infants
- No measurement/reporting of antimicrobial resistance emergence
- Dose/regimen details not provided in the abstract
