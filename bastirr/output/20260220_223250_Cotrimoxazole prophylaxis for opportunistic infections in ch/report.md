# Systematic Review Report

## 1. Review Summary
- **Title:** Cotrimoxazole prophylaxis for opportunistic infections in children with HIV infection
- **Objective:** To assess the effects of routinely administered cotrimoxazole on death and illness episodes in children with HIV infection, and in infants of HIV‐infected mothers.

## 2. Search Results
- Number of candidate studies found: 10

## 3. Screening Results
- Included studies: 1
- Excluded studies: 9
- Decisions:
  - Study 1: INCLUDE — Double-blind randomized placebo-controlled trial of cotrimoxazole prophylaxis vs placebo in HIV-infected children aged 1–14 years; matches pediatric population (<15 y), intervention (routine cotrimoxazole), comparison (placebo), and randomized design.
  - Study 2: EXCLUDE — Adult-only randomized trial; pediatric/infant eligibility not met; excludes by population.
  - Study 3: EXCLUDE — Randomized/no-therapy study in adults with AIDS (Kaposi's sarcoma); not pediatric; excludes by population.
  - Study 4: EXCLUDE — Adult case series (observational) in homosexual men; not a randomized prophylaxis trial; excludes by design and population.
  - Study 5: EXCLUDE — Observational necropsy study in young children; no randomized allocation to cotrimoxazole prophylaxis; excludes by design.
  - Study 6: EXCLUDE — Prospective cohort of children; no randomized comparison of cotrimoxazole prophylaxis; excludes by design.
  - Study 7: EXCLUDE — Prospective observational study; no randomized prophylaxis allocation; excludes by design.
  - Study 8: EXCLUDE — Randomized trial in adult HIV-infected TB patients (median age ~32); excludes by population.
  - Study 9: EXCLUDE — Narrative/review on mother-to-child transmission; not a randomized prophylaxis trial; excludes by design.
  - Study 10: EXCLUDE — Prospective diagnostic/epidemiologic study in HIV-infected children; prophylaxis use not randomized; excludes by design.

## 4. Evidence Extraction Summary
**Study 1** — Cotrimoxazole as prophylaxis against opportunistic infections in HIV‐infected Zambian children (CHAP): a double‐blind randomised placebo‐controlled trial
- **Population:** Children aged 1–14 years with clinical features of HIV infection in Zambia; 541 randomized, 7 subsequently identified as HIV-negative and excluded (HIV-infected children analyzed).
- **Setting:** Zambia; resource-poor setting with high levels of bacterial resistance to co-trimoxazole; pediatric outpatient/clinical trial context.
- **Intervention:** Daily co-trimoxazole (trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole) prophylaxis; dosing not specified in abstract.
- **Comparator:** Placebo.
- **Outcomes:**
  - All-cause mortality (intention-to-treat; median follow-up 19 months): HR 0.57 (95% CI 0.43–0.77); p=0.0002
  - All-cause mortality among children followed beyond 12 months (n=320): HR 0.48 (95% CI 0.27–0.84)
  - Severe adverse events (grade 3 or 4) — comparative incidence
  - Neutrophil count <0.5×10^9/L (on one occasion) — comparative incidence; p=0.06
  - Pneumocystis carinii detection among children with pneumonia (nasopharyngeal aspirates)
- **Adverse events:**
  - Grade 3 or 4 adverse events: 16/ (6%) co-trimoxazole vs 18/ (7%) placebo.
  - Rash: 1 event (placebo).
  - Neutrophil count <0.5×10^9/L on one occasion: 16 (6%) co-trimoxazole vs 7 (3%) placebo; p=0.06.
- **Study design:** Double-blind randomized placebo-controlled trial (RCT); analysis by intention-to-treat; early stopping by DSMC.
- **Follow-up:** Median follow-up 19 months; subgroup analysis for >12 months (n=320).
- **Key findings:**
  - Mortality: 74 deaths (28%) in co-trimoxazole vs 112 deaths (42%) in placebo; HR 0.57 (95% CI 0.43–0.77), p=0.0002.
  - Mortality benefit persisted beyond 12 months: HR 0.48 (95% CI 0.27–0.84) among n=320 followed >12 months; test for heterogeneity p=0.60.
  - Effect consistent across ages (test for heterogeneity p=0.82) and baseline CD4 counts (test for heterogeneity p=0.36).
  - Severe (grade 3/4) adverse events similar between groups: 6% co-trimoxazole vs 7% placebo.
  - Neutropenia <0.5×10^9/L: 6% co-trimoxazole vs 3% placebo; p=0.06.
  - Pneumocystis carinii detected by immunofluorescence in 1/73 nasopharyngeal aspirates (placebo group) among children with pneumonia.
- **Notes:** Trial conducted in Zambia with high background bacterial resistance to co-trimoxazole; early stopping recommended by DSMC in Oct 2003; daily prophylaxis regimen; intention-to-treat analysis; placebo-controlled; double-blind.


## 5. Synthesis & Conclusions
One double-blind randomized placebo-controlled trial in Zambia evaluated daily cotrimoxazole prophylaxis in HIV-infected children aged 1–14 years [1]. Despite high background bacterial resistance, cotrimoxazole significantly reduced all-cause mortality compared with placebo over a median 19 months. Event counts were 74 deaths in cotrimoxazole versus 112 in placebo. The mortality benefit persisted among children followed beyond 12 months (HR 0.48, 95% CI 0.27–0.84) [1]. Benefits were consistent across age groups and baseline CD4 strata, suggesting broad efficacy across pediatric subgroups within the trial [1]. Illness episodes were not systematically quantified in the abstracted data; the focus was mortality and severe adverse events. Thus, direct estimates on non-fatal morbidity were not reported. Safety was generally comparable between groups. Grade 3/4 adverse events occurred in 16 children (6%) on cotrimoxazole versus 18 (7%) on placebo. Severe neutropenia (<0.5×10^9/L on one occasion) occurred in 16 (6%) vs 7 (3%); p=0.06, indicating a borderline hematologic signal. No included study directly evaluated routine cotrimoxazole in infants of HIV-infected mothers (e.g., HIV-exposed uninfected infants); applicability to that group is therefore indirect within this evidence set. The trial was conducted in a resource-limited setting with high antimicrobial resistance, supporting relevance where bacterial infections are common. However, early stopping by the DSMC may inflate effect estimates, and the pre-ART or limited ART context reduces certainty about generalizability to current programs with widespread ART access [1]. Implications for practice in low-resource settings: Where ART access is limited or bacterial infection burden is high, routine cotrimoxazole prophylaxis in HIV-infected children is likely to reduce mortality with acceptable safety. Programs should monitor for hematologic toxicity (e.g., neutropenia) and reassess benefits as ART coverage and epidemiology evolve.

## 6. Quality Assessment
- **Study 1:** Quality rating — moderate
  - Strength: Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled design
  - Strength: Intention-to-treat analysis
  - Strength: Large relative reduction in all-cause mortality with reasonably tight confidence intervals
  - Strength: Effects consistent across age groups and baseline CD4 strata
  - Strength: Conducted in a high-burden, resource-limited setting with high background bacterial resistance
  - Limitation: Early stopping by Data and Safety Monitoring Committee, which can overestimate treatment effects
  - Limitation: Sequence generation and allocation concealment not fully detailed in the abstract
  - Limitation: Dosing regimen not specified in the abstract
  - Limitation: No detailed reporting of illness episode counts; primary focus on mortality and severe adverse events
  - Limitation: Single-country study; external generalizability beyond similar settings uncertain
  - Limitation: Borderline higher severe neutropenia (p=0.06) suggests possible hematologic toxicity
  - Limitation: Likely pre-ART or limited ART access context; applicability to ART-era programs uncertain
  - Bias concern: Early stopping for benefit
  - Bias concern: Insufficient reporting of allocation concealment in the abstract
  - Bias concern: Minor enrollment misclassification (7 later found HIV-negative and excluded from HIV-infected analysis)
  - Applicability: direct

**Overall evidence strength:** moderate

**Key limitations:**
- Single RCT from one country and one health-system context
- Early stopping for benefit may overestimate treatment effect
- Limited reporting of allocation concealment and dosing details in abstract
- Lack of detailed illness episode outcomes; focus on mortality and severe adverse events
- Pre-ART or limited ART context; applicability to current ART-era settings uncertain
- No direct evidence for infants of HIV-infected mothers within the included studies
- Setting with high local resistance may not reflect all regions
