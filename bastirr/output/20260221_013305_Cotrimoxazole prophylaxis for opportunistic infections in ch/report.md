# Systematic Review Report

## 1. Review Summary
- **Title:** Cotrimoxazole prophylaxis for opportunistic infections in children with HIV infection
- **Objective:** To assess the effects of routinely administered cotrimoxazole on death and illness episodes in children with HIV infection, and in infants of HIV‐infected mothers.

## 4. Evidence Extraction Summary
**Study 1** — CHAP Trial (Zambia) 2003
- **Population:** Children aged 1–14 years with clinical features of HIV infection; Zambia. 541 randomized; 7 later found HIV-negative and excluded from analyses; intention-to-treat analysis otherwise.
- **Setting:** Zambia; resource-poor setting with high background bacterial resistance to cotrimoxazole.
- **Intervention:** Daily cotrimoxazole (trimethoprim–sulfamethoxazole) prophylaxis; dose not reported in abstract; initiated at enrollment; median follow-up 19 months.
- **Comparator:** Placebo (matching), standard care otherwise.
- **Outcomes:**
  - All-cause mortality (overall): HR 0.57 (95% CI 0.43–0.77); p=0.0002
  - All-cause mortality (>12 months follow-up subgroup): HR 0.48 (95% CI 0.27–0.84)
  - Any grade 3/4 adverse event (possibly treatment-related)
  - Severe neutropenia (ANC <0.5×10^9/L on one occasion); p=0.06
  - Rash
  - PCP organism identified among pneumonia aspirates (n=73 tested)
- **Adverse events:**
  - Grade 3/4 adverse events: 16/≈co-trimoxazole group (6%) vs 18/≈placebo (7%).
  - Severe neutropenia (<0.5×10^9/L on one occasion): 16 (6%) co-trimoxazole vs 7 (3%) placebo; p=0.06.
  - Rash: 1 case in placebo group.
- **Study design:** Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial (RCT). Allocation concealment not reported in abstract.
- **Follow-up:** Median 19 months; benefit persisted beyond 12 months.
- **Key findings:**
  - Mortality reduced with cotrimoxazole vs placebo: HR 0.57 (95% CI 0.43–0.77), p=0.0002.
  - Mortality benefit also observed among those followed >12 months: HR 0.48 (95% CI 0.27–0.84).
  - Effect consistent across age groups (heterogeneity p=0.82) and baseline CD4 counts (heterogeneity p=0.36).
  - Trial stopped early (Oct 2003) by DSMB due to mortality benefit.
  - Severe neutropenia was more frequent in cotrimoxazole group; difference not statistically significant (p=0.06).
  - PCP organism detected in only 1 of 73 pneumonia aspirates (placebo group), suggesting low PCP identification in this setting.
- **Notes:** Primary outcomes: mortality and adverse events possibly related to treatment. Antiretroviral therapy (ART) exposure not reported in abstract; likely limited (pre-ART era context). Allocation concealment not reported. Antimicrobial resistance to cotrimoxazole was high in the setting but resistance as an outcome was not measured. Funding/conflicts not stated.


## 5. Synthesis & Conclusions
One randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in Zambia evaluated daily cotrimoxazole prophylaxis among HIV-infected children aged 1–14 years [1]. The setting had high background bacterial resistance to cotrimoxazole, median follow-up was 19 months, and the trial was stopped early by the DSMB for benefit [1]. All-cause mortality was significantly lower with cotrimoxazole versus placebo (HR 0.57, 95% CI 0.43–0.77; p=0.0002) [1]. The mortality benefit persisted beyond 12 months of follow-up (HR 0.48, 95% CI 0.27–0.84) [1]. Effects were consistent across age categories and baseline CD4 strata with no evidence of heterogeneity [1]. Overall illness episode rates were not comprehensively reported in the abstract, limiting inference on morbidity [1]. Among pneumonia aspirates tested, Pneumocystis jirovecii was detected in only 1 of 73 samples (placebo arm), suggesting low identified PCP in this cohort; however, this does not substitute for a systematic assessment of overall illness episodes [1]. Secondary outcomes were incompletely reported. Opportunistic infections, serious bacterial infections, and hospitalization rates were not systematically presented [1]. Safety profiles were broadly similar between groups for grade 3/4 adverse events (approximately 6% vs 7%). Severe neutropenia occurred more often with cotrimoxazole (6% vs 3%; p≈0.06), indicating a possible safety signal warranting monitoring [1]. Antimicrobial resistance outcomes were not measured despite high background resistance in the setting [1]. Applicability: The evidence is direct for HIV-infected children in resource-limited, largely pre-ART contexts [1]. It does not include HIV-exposed but uninfected infants, and ART exposure was not reported, limiting applicability to contemporary ART-era settings. Nevertheless, the observed mortality reduction despite high background cotrimoxazole resistance supports biological plausibility and potential benefit where bacterial infections drive mortality before or during early ART initiation [1]. Heterogeneity and consistency: With only one included RCT, between-study heterogeneity cannot be assessed. Internal consistency across age and CD4 subgroups is reassuring, but early stopping may overestimate effect sizes [1]. Implications for practice: In HIV-infected children in resource-limited, pre-ART settings, routine cotrimoxazole prophylaxis likely reduces all-cause mortality with acceptable safety, while monitoring for neutropenia is prudent [1]. In ART-era programs, cotrimoxazole may provide benefits prior to and during early ART, but direct contemporary evidence in virally suppressed children is limited. Implications for research: High-priority studies should evaluate cotrimoxazole in ART-era pediatric populations (including infants of HIV-infected mothers), measure morbidity (overall illness episodes, serious bacterial infections, and hospitalizations), antimicrobial resistance, dosing and duration, and define stopping rules in the context of immune reconstitution.

## 6. Quality Assessment
- **Study 1:** Quality rating — moderate
  - Strength: Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled design
  - Strength: Intention-to-treat analysis (except for confirmed HIV-negative exclusions)
  - Strength: Consistent mortality benefit across age and baseline CD4 subgroups
  - Strength: Independent DSMB oversight
  - Limitation: Allocation concealment not reported
  - Limitation: Trial stopped early for benefit (risk of overestimating effect size)
  - Limitation: Antiretroviral therapy exposure not reported (likely pre-ART era)
  - Limitation: Dosing details not reported in abstract
  - Limitation: Limited reporting of overall illness episodes and key morbidity outcomes
  - Limitation: No antimicrobial resistance outcomes reported
  - Limitation: Single-country setting may limit generalizability
  - Bias concern: Allocation concealment: unclear
  - Bias concern: Early stopping for benefit may inflate effect estimates
  - Bias concern: Selective reporting possible (limited morbidity and hospitalization reporting)
  - Bias concern: Incomplete transparency on randomization procedures in abstract
  - Applicability: direct

**Overall evidence strength:** moderate

**Key limitations:**
- Only one randomized controlled trial available
- Pre-ART era context limits applicability to current ART-era settings
- Allocation concealment not reported
- Trial stopped early for benefit (possible overestimation of effect)
- Inadequate reporting of overall illness episodes and key morbidity outcomes
- No antimicrobial resistance outcomes reported
- ART exposure and adherence details not reported
- Single-country study limits generalizability
