5. Synthesis & Conclusions

Mortality: In a double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled trial from Zambia, daily cotrimoxazole prophylaxis reduced all-cause mortality versus placebo (74 vs 112 deaths; HR 0.57, 95% CI 0.43–0.77; p=0.0002) with consistency across age groups and baseline CD4 strata and a stronger effect in those followed beyond 12 months (HR 0.48, 95% CI 0.27–0.84) [1]. Illness episodes and hospitalizations: Not reported in the abstract, representing an evidence gap for morbidity outcomes [1]. Safety: Overall grade 3/4 adverse events occurred at similar frequencies (6% cotrimoxazole vs 7% placebo). Severe neutropenia (<0.5×10^9/L on one occasion) was more frequent with cotrimoxazole (6% vs 3%; p=0.06), a borderline difference that warrants monitoring; rash was rare (1 case in placebo) [1]. Evidence gaps: No direct evidence for infants of HIV-infected mothers; illness episodes and hospitalization data were not reported; dosing details were not specified; longer-term safety and antimicrobial resistance implications remain uncertain [1]. Overall, based on a single moderate-quality RCT, evidence is moderate for mortality reduction with routine cotrimoxazole in HIV-infected children, while evidence is insufficient for effects on illness episodes and limited for detailed safety outcomes [1].

6. Quality Assessment (evidence strength, limitations)

- Study 1 (CHAP): Overall quality rating: moderate.

  Risk of bias:
  - Randomization: Some concerns (sequence generation not reported in abstract).
  - Allocation concealment: Unclear.
  - Blinding: Low risk (double-blind, placebo-controlled; mortality is objective).
  - Attrition: Low to some concerns (ITT reported; loss to follow-up details not in abstract).
  - Selective reporting: Some concerns (illness episodes/hospitalizations not reported).
  - Early stopping: Some concerns (stopped early for benefit, may inflate effect sizes).

  Strengths: Double-blind RCT, ITT, consistent mortality benefit across subgroups, relevant high-resistance, resource-limited setting.

  Limitations: Randomization/concealment details unclear; early stopping; limited morbidity/safety detail; no infants of HIV-infected mothers.
