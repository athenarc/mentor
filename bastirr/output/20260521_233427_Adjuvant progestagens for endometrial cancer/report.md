# Systematic Review Report

- **Title:** Adjuvant progestagens for endometrial cancer
- **Objective:** To evaluate the effectiveness and safety of adjuvant progestagen therapy for the treatment of endometrial cancer.

## 4. Evidence Extraction Summary
**Study 1** — Treatment of FIGO (1971) stage 1 endometrial carcinoma with intensive surgery, radiotherapy and hormonotherapy according to pathological prognostic groups. Long term results of a randomised multicentre trial (authors/year not stated in abstract)
- **Population:** Patients with apparent FIGO (1971) Stage I endometrial carcinoma; subgroups by pathological extent: disease limited to endometrium; inner myometrial invasion with well/moderate differentiation; moderate/deep myometrial invasion or undifferentiated grade; node-positive (N+). Histology not specified. Age not reported.
- **Setting:** Randomised multicentre trial; postoperative management based on pathological extension; intention-to-treat analysis at 84 months on 856 patients.
- **Intervention:** Adjuvant medroxyprogesterone acetate (MPA) 100 mg orally twice daily for 12 months; given alone in some strata or combined with pelvic (± para-aortic) radiotherapy depending on risk group.
- **Comparator:** Risk-adapted controls: (1) No further treatment vs MPA for inner myometrial invasion with well/moderate differentiation; (2) Pelvic radiotherapy vs pelvic radiotherapy + MPA for moderate/deep invasion or undifferentiated grade; (3) Pelvic + para-aortic radiotherapy vs the same + MPA for node-positive disease.
- **Outcomes:**
  - Relapse-free survival
  - Overall survival
  - Recurrence
- **Adverse events:** Not reported.
- **Study design:** RCT
- **Follow-up:** 84 months (7 years), intention-to-treat analysis on 856 patients.
- **Key findings:**
  - High relapse-free survival overall at 84 months.
  - No significant difference between MPA-treated and untreated groups in relapse-free survival.
  - Adjuvant hormonotherapy (MPA) did not improve cure rate when added to risk-adapted treatment.
- **Notes:** Risk-stratified randomization by pathological extension; multiple comparator structures per stratum. No effect sizes (HR/RR/OR) or CIs reported in abstract.

**Study 5** — A randomised trial of adjuvant progestagens in early endometrial cancer (authors/year not stated in abstract)
- **Population:** Stage I or Stage II endometrial cancer after surgery; N=1148 randomized. High-risk subgroup N=461 mentioned. Histology and age not reported.
- **Setting:** Randomized controlled trial; postoperative adjuvant context.
- **Intervention:** Adjuvant progestagen (progesterone); dose/schedule/duration not reported.
- **Comparator:** Observation/no additional therapy after surgery.
- **Outcomes:**
  - Overall survival (crude survival)
  - Relapse rate / Disease-free survival
  - Death due to intercurrent disease (non-cancer mortality); p=0.04
  - Median survival among cancer-related deaths; p=0.03
- **Adverse events:**
  - Higher death due to intercurrent disease in the progestagen group (P=0.04).
- **Study design:** RCT
- **Follow-up:** 42–132 months (median 72 months).
- **Key findings:**
  - Crude overall survival and relapse rates were similar between groups.
  - Non-cancer (intercurrent) mortality was higher in the progestagen group (P=0.04).
  - Among those with cancer-related death, median survival was longer in the progestagen group (30 vs 22 months; P=0.03).
  - In 461 high-risk patients, a trend toward fewer cancer-related deaths and better disease-free survival with progestagen was observed, but crude survival was unchanged.
- **Notes:** Dose/schedule/duration of progesterone not reported. No HR/RR/OR or CIs reported. Findings include subgroup tendencies but no definitive survival benefit overall.

**Study 7** — A randomised trial of progestagens in the primary treatment of endometrial carcinoma (authors/year not stated in abstract)
- **Population:** Endometrial carcinoma patients (clinical stage not specified); N=429 randomized at diagnosis. Histology and age not reported.
- **Setting:** Regional randomized trial in Yorkshire (UK); progestogens evaluated as part of primary treatment alongside surgery and radiotherapy.
- **Intervention:** Progestogens as part of primary treatment in conjunction with surgery and radiotherapy; agent/dose/schedule/duration not reported.
- **Comparator:** Primary treatment (surgery ± radiotherapy) without added progestogen.
- **Outcomes:**
  - Overall survival (projected 5-year)
- **Adverse events:** Not reported.
- **Study design:** RCT
- **Follow-up:** All patients >1 year post-operation; >50% >5 years after operation; projected overall 5-year survival 76% (overall).
- **Key findings:**
  - No statistically significant difference in overall survival between progestogen and control groups.
  - Result persisted after adjustment for known prognostic factors.
  - Projected overall 5-year survival for the cohort was 76%.
- **Notes:** Evaluates progestogens as part of primary treatment, not strictly adjuvant-only. No effect sizes (HR/RR/OR) or CIs reported.

**Study 10** — Adjuvant progestagen therapy improves survival in patients with endometrial cancer after hysterectomy. Results of one-institutional prospective clinical trial (authors/year not stated in abstract)
- **Population:** Post-hysterectomy endometrial cancer patients; N=205 randomized. Stage, histology, and age not reported.
- **Setting:** Single-institution randomized prospective clinical trial (postoperative).
- **Intervention:** Adjuvant progestagen therapy; agent/dose/schedule/duration not reported.
- **Comparator:** No additional therapy (observation) after surgery.
- **Outcomes:**
  - Overall survival; p=0.001
- **Adverse events:** Not reported.
- **Study design:** RCT
- **Follow-up:** 5 years.
- **Key findings:**
  - Progestagen group had significantly longer survival than control (log-rank test; P < 0.001).
- **Notes:** Only p-value reported; no HR estimates or CIs. P-value entered as 0.001 to reflect P<0.001 in abstract.


## 5. Synthesis & Conclusions
Effectiveness: Across the larger randomized evidence, adjuvant progestagens did not improve overall survival (OS) or relapse-/disease-free survival (DFS/RFS). The multicentre, risk-stratified Stage I RCT [1] reported high relapse-free survival overall at 84 months but no difference between medroxyprogesterone acetate (MPA) and controls, concluding that hormonotherapy did not improve cure rates. The very large early-stage adjuvant RCT [5] also found similar crude OS and relapse/DFS rates between progestagen and observation. A regional RCT that tested progestogens as part of primary therapy (not purely adjuvant) [7] likewise showed no statistically significant OS difference after adjustment for prognostic factors, with a projected 5-year OS of 76% overall. In contrast, a smaller single-institution postoperative RCT [10] reported significantly longer survival with adjuvant progestagen (log-rank P<0.001), but sparse reporting, small sample size, and single-center design raise concerns about internal validity. On balance, the totality of evidence—weighted by the larger multicentre trials—does not support an OS or DFS/RFS benefit for routine adjuvant progestagen therapy.

Recurrence: Consistent with DFS/RFS findings, [1] and [5] report no reduction in relapse/recurrence with adjuvant progestagens in the overall populations. Study [5] described a tendency toward fewer cancer-related deaths and improved disease-free survival in a high-risk subgroup (n=461), but this did not translate into an OS advantage, and definitive effect sizes/CIs were not provided.

Safety: Adverse event reporting is limited. The large RCT [5] detected higher non-cancer (intercurrent) mortality in the progestagen arm (P=0.04), without further safety detail. Other trials either did not report adverse events or provided insufficient data to characterize harms. Given the absence of benefit and a potential safety signal for increased intercurrent deaths, the risk–benefit balance appears unfavorable for routine use.

Heterogeneity and applicability: Heterogeneity arises from differences in treatment context and co-interventions. Study [1] risk-adapted treatments and combined MPA with radiotherapy in higher-risk strata, potentially diluting a drug-specific effect and complicating cross-stratum comparisons. Study [7] tested progestogens as part of primary therapy (with surgery and radiotherapy), rendering it indirect to an adjuvant-only question. Agent, dose, and duration were rarely reported outside [1], limiting comparability and dose–response inference. Despite these differences, the larger body of randomized evidence remains consistent in showing no OS/DFS advantage for adjuvant progestagens.

Practice implications: Based on moderate-certainty evidence dominated by large multicentre RCTs [1], [5] with neutral results and a concerning intercurrent mortality signal [5], routine adjuvant progestagen therapy after surgery for endometrial cancer is not supported. If considered at all, it should be restricted to clinical trials or exceptional scenarios where trial participation is not feasible, with careful patient counseling about uncertain benefit and potential harms. Existing positive findings from a small, single-institution study [10] should not change practice in the absence of corroboration from larger, well-reported trials.

Research implications: Modern, adequately powered RCTs are needed, with standardized agents/doses/durations, contemporary staging and radiotherapy/chemotherapy backbones, molecular and histologic stratification (e.g., grade, PR status, molecular subtypes), robust time-to-event reporting (HRs with CIs), competing risk analyses, comprehensive adverse event and cardiovascular/thromboembolic monitoring, and quality-of-life outcomes. Focused evaluation in pre-specified high-risk groups is warranted, given suggestive—but inconclusive—signals in [5].

## 6. Quality Assessment
- **Study 1:** Quality rating — moderate
  - Strength: Randomized multicentre design
  - Strength: Large sample size (n=856) with intention-to-treat analysis
  - Strength: Long follow-up (84 months)
  - Strength: Pre-specified pathological risk strata
  - Limitation: Allocation concealment and blinding not reported
  - Limitation: Comparator and co-interventions vary across strata (risk-adapted), introducing heterogeneity
  - Limitation: Effect sizes and 95% CIs not reported
  - Limitation: Adverse events not reported; limited safety characterization
  - Limitation: Insufficient detail on histology/age distribution
  - Bias concern: Selection bias due to unclear randomization/allocation concealment
  - Bias concern: Performance/detection bias (no blinding; although outcomes objective)
  - Bias concern: Selective reporting (no HRs/ORs/CIs reported)
  - Bias concern: Potential confounding from non-uniform co-interventions across strata
  - Applicability: direct
- **Study 5:** Quality rating — moderate
  - Strength: Randomized design with very large sample (n=1148)
  - Strength: Long median follow-up (~72 months)
  - Strength: Explicit reporting of intercurrent mortality signal
  - Strength: Exploration of high-risk subgroup (n=461)
  - Limitation: Dose/schedule/duration of progestagen not reported
  - Limitation: Key effect measures (HR/RR/OR) and CIs not reported; reliance on crude survival
  - Limitation: Adverse events only partially reported; mechanism for intercurrent mortality increase unclear
  - Limitation: Potential selective outcome reporting (subset analyses without comprehensive estimates)
  - Limitation: Lack of details on histology, staging breakdown, and co-interventions
  - Bias concern: Selection bias (allocation concealment not described)
  - Bias concern: Performance/detection bias (no blinding reported)
  - Bias concern: Selective reporting bias (absence of effect sizes/CIs; reliance on P-values)
  - Bias concern: Competing risk handling unclear for DFS/OS
  - Applicability: direct
- **Study 7:** Quality rating — low
  - Strength: Randomized design
  - Strength: Moderate sample size (n=429)
  - Strength: Mature follow-up (majority >5 years)
  - Limitation: Evaluates progestogens as part of primary treatment (surgery ± RT), not strictly adjuvant; indirect to review objective
  - Limitation: Stage and histology details not reported
  - Limitation: No effect sizes or CIs; only projected 5-year OS
  - Limitation: Adverse events not reported
  - Limitation: Agent, dose, schedule, duration not described
  - Bias concern: Selection bias (allocation concealment not reported)
  - Bias concern: Performance/detection bias (no blinding reported)
  - Bias concern: Reporting bias (limited outcomes and metrics)
  - Bias concern: Potential imbalance in co-interventions not detailed
  - Applicability: indirect
- **Study 10:** Quality rating — low
  - Strength: Randomized design
  - Strength: Single-institution setting may standardize certain co-interventions
  - Strength: Five-year follow-up
  - Limitation: Small sample size (n=205) and single-center design
  - Limitation: Only a P-value reported (no HRs/CIs); insufficient statistical detail
  - Limitation: Baseline comparability, allocation concealment, and blinding not described
  - Limitation: Adverse events not reported
  - Limitation: Agent/dose/schedule/duration not reported
  - Bias concern: Selection bias (allocation concealment unclear; small single-center RCT vulnerable to imbalance)
  - Bias concern: Performance/detection bias (no blinding)
  - Bias concern: Selective reporting bias (no effect sizes or safety data)
  - Bias concern: Potential random error and center effects
  - Applicability: direct

**Overall evidence strength:** moderate

**Key limitations:**
- Sparse reporting of effect sizes (HR/RR/OR) and 95% CIs across trials; reliance on P-values and crude survival
- Inadequate adverse event reporting; limited insight into causes of increased intercurrent mortality
- Heterogeneous interventions/comparators (e.g., risk-adapted RT ± progestagen; primary treatment context in [7])
- Lack of standardized progestagen agent/dose/duration in most studies
- Incomplete baseline/staging/histology details; older staging systems and treatment era
- Single-center positive outlier [10] with potential high risk of bias and small sample size
