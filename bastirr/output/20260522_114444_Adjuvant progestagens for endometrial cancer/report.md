# Systematic Review Report

## 1. Review Summary
- **Title:** Adjuvant progestagens for endometrial cancer
- **Objective:** To evaluate the effectiveness and safety of adjuvant progestagen therapy for the treatment of endometrial cancer.

## 2. Search Results
- Number of candidate studies found: 10

## 3. Screening Results
- Included studies: 5
- Excluded studies: 5
- Decisions:
  - Study 1: INCLUDE — Multicentre randomised controlled trial comparing adjuvant MPA (progestagen) versus no further treatment or radiotherapy alone after surgery in women with stage I endometrial cancer. Satisfies all selection criteria.
  - Study 2: EXCLUDE — This is a narrative review of clinical observations, not a randomised controlled trial. Study design does not meet inclusion criteria.
  - Study 3: EXCLUDE — Methodological article about meta-analysis; not a randomised controlled trial.
  - Study 4: EXCLUDE — Book chapter on statistical methods for meta-analysis; not a randomised controlled trial.
  - Study 5: INCLUDE — Large RCT (n=1148) comparing adjuvant progesterone versus no additional therapy after surgery for Stage I/II endometrial cancer. Meets all criteria.
  - Study 6: EXCLUDE — Methodological paper discussing random effects meta-analysis; not a randomised controlled trial.
  - Study 7: INCLUDE — Randomised trial assessing progestogen therapy in conjunction with surgery and radiotherapy for endometrial carcinoma. Although described as 'primary treatment', the therapy was used in addition to surgery (likely adjuvant) and the study population includes women who underwent surgery. Included conservatively; may require full-text assessment to confirm adjuvant setting.
  - Study 8: EXCLUDE — Methodological paper on extracting summary statistics for meta-analysis; not an RCT.
  - Study 9: INCLUDE — Small RCT (n=35) comparing adjuvant hydroxyprogesterone after surgery for stage I endometrial cancer versus placebo. Meets criteria.
  - Study 10: INCLUDE — Prospective RCT (n=205) comparing adjuvant progestagen therapy versus no additional therapy after hysterectomy for endometrial cancer. Meets all criteria.

## 4. Evidence Extraction Summary
**Study 1** — Not provided (multicentre trial 1990s)
- **Population:** 856 patients with apparent FIGO stage I endometrial carcinoma, stratified by pathological risk groups (myometrial invasion, differentiation, nodal status)
- **Setting:** Multicentre trial
- **Intervention:** Adjuvant medroxyprogesterone acetate (MPA) 100 mg orally twice daily for 12 months post-surgery
- **Comparator:** No further treatment (low-risk group: inner myometrial invasion, well/moderate differentiation); radiotherapy alone vs radiotherapy + MPA (higher-risk groups: moderate/deep invasion or undifferentiated; node-positive)
- **Outcomes:**
  - Relapse-free survival
- **Adverse events:** Not reported
- **Study design:** RCT (multicentre, risk-stratified)
- **Follow-up:** 84 months
- **Key findings:**
  - High relapse-free survival overall, but no significant difference between MPA-treated and untreated groups
  - Adjuvant hormonotherapy does not improve cure rate
  - Relapse-free survival influenced by treatment based on pathological extension, not by MPA
- **Notes:** Risk-stratified randomisation; MPA duration 12 months; analysis by intention-to-treat. Exact p-values not provided in abstract.

**Study 5** — Not provided (randomised trial, 1990s-2000s)
- **Population:** 1148 patients with Stage I or II endometrial cancer after surgery
- **Setting:** Not specified (likely multicentre)
- **Intervention:** Adjuvant progestagen (progesterone) therapy
- **Comparator:** No additional therapy
- **Outcomes:**
  - Crude survival
  - Relapse rate
  - Death due to intercurrent disease; p=0.04
  - Median survival (cancer-related death): 8.0; p=0.03
  - Disease-free survival (high-risk subgroup, n=461)
- **Adverse events:** Death due to intercurrent disease significantly higher in progesterone group (P=0.04)
- **Study design:** RCT
- **Follow-up:** 42–132 months (median 72 months)
- **Key findings:**
  - Crude survival and relapse rates similar between groups
  - Higher intercurrent death in progestagen group (P=0.04)
  - Median survival for cancer-related deaths longer in progestagen group (30 vs 22 months, P=0.03)
  - High-risk subgroup showed tendency towards fewer cancer-related deaths and better disease-free survival, but no improvement in crude survival
  - Authors conclude little benefit for low-risk patients; further studies needed in high-risk
- **Notes:** Effect size for median survival difference is the absolute difference in months (progestagen 30, control 22).

**Study 7** — Not provided (Yorkshire randomised trial, 1980s)
- **Population:** 429 patients with endometrial carcinoma receiving primary treatment (surgery and radiotherapy)
- **Setting:** Yorkshire, UK
- **Intervention:** Progestogens added to primary treatment (surgery + radiotherapy)
- **Comparator:** Control group without progestogens
- **Outcomes:**
  - 5-year overall survival
- **Adverse events:** Not reported
- **Study design:** RCT
- **Follow-up:** All patients >1 year; over half >5 years post-operation
- **Key findings:**
  - Projected 5-year survival rate 76% overall
  - No statistically significant difference in survival between progestogen and control groups, even after adjustment for known prognostic factors
  - Contrary to expectation, progestogens did not improve survival
- **Notes:** Exact p-value not provided; authors state not significant after adjustment.

**Study 9** — Not provided (small RCT, 1970s-1980s)
- **Population:** 35 patients with stage IA or IB endometrial carcinoma, treated with total abdominal hysterectomy and bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy
- **Setting:** Not specified (likely single centre)
- **Intervention:** 6-methyl-17α-hydroxyprogesterone within 24 hours after surgery, then weekly for 14 weeks
- **Comparator:** Placebo on identical schedule
- **Outcomes:**
  - Survival (5-year)
  - Recurrence rate
- **Adverse events:** Cancer deaths: 2 in hormone group, 1 in placebo group
- **Study design:** RCT (placebo-controlled)
- **Follow-up:** 5 years (all 35 patients completed follow-up)
- **Key findings:**
  - No improvement in survival with hormone therapy
  - No influence on recurrence rate
  - Small sample size limits power
- **Notes:** Very small sample (n=35). Therapy initiated within 24h post-op; duration 14 weeks.

**Study 10** — Not provided (prospective single-institution trial, 2000s)
- **Population:** 205 patients with endometrial cancer after hysterectomy
- **Setting:** Single institution
- **Intervention:** Adjuvant progestagen (referred to as progestaten) treatment
- **Comparator:** No additional therapy
- **Outcomes:**
  - Overall survival; p=0.001
- **Adverse events:** Not reported
- **Study design:** Prospective RCT
- **Follow-up:** 5 years
- **Key findings:**
  - Significantly longer survival in progestagen group (Log-rank test, P < 0.001)
  - Authors concluded evidence of gain from adjuvant progestagen therapy
- **Notes:** P value < 0.001 indicates statistically significant survival benefit, in contrast to most other trials. Study details limited in abstract.


## 5. Synthesis & Conclusions
This review includes five randomised controlled trials evaluating adjuvant progestagen therapy for endometrial cancer, encompassing a total of 2,683 patients. The evidence is dominated by three multicentre RCTs (Studies 1, 5, and 7) with sample sizes ranging from 429 to 1148, and two smaller trials (Studies 9 and 10).

Overall survival and relapse-free survival: Four of the five trials found no statistically significant improvement in survival. In Study 1 (n=856), adjuvant medroxyprogesterone acetate (MPA) for 12 months did not improve relapse-free survival at 84 months, and the authors concluded that adjuvant hormonotherapy does not improve cure rates [1]. Study 5 (n=1148) similarly showed no difference in crude survival or relapse rates between the progestagen and control groups. However, a high-risk subgroup (n=461) showed a tendency towards fewer cancer-related deaths and better disease-free survival, although crude survival was unchanged [2]. Study 7 (n=429) reported a projected 5-year overall survival of 76% with no significant difference between groups after adjusting for prognostic factors [3]. Study 9 (n=35), a small placebo-controlled trial of hydroxyprogesterone for 14 weeks, found no improvement in survival or recurrence [4]. In contrast, Study 10 (n=205), a single-institution RCT, reported a statistically significant survival benefit for adjuvant progestagen (P<0.001) [5].

Cancer-related survival: Study 5 provided more detailed outcome data, showing that while intercurrent deaths were significantly higher in the progestagen group (P=0.04), the median survival among those who died of cancer was longer in the progestagen group (30 months vs 22 months, P=0.03) [2]. This suggests a potential differential effect on cancer-specific outcomes, although the increase in non-cancer deaths raises safety concerns.

Safety: Only Study 5 reported a safety signal—a higher rate of death due to intercurrent disease in the progestagen group (P=0.04). The other studies did not report adverse events, limiting the ability to assess overall safety. Study 9 noted cancer deaths (2 in hormone group vs 1 in placebo) but with such low numbers no conclusions can be drawn [4].

Methodological considerations: The quality of the evidence is mixed. The larger multicentre trials (Studies 1, 5, 7) provide the most reliable estimates but suffer from incomplete reporting of effect measures and p-values. Study 5 is the most informative, reporting multiple outcomes and subgroup data, but still lacks hazard ratios. Study 10, the only strongly positive trial, is at high risk of bias due to its single-centre design and limited methodological detail. The small sample of Study 9 precludes meaningful conclusions. Overall, the evidence is inconsistent: the largest and most robust trials do not show a clear survival benefit, while one smaller, higher risk-of-bias study suggests a benefit.

In summary, the available evidence does not convincingly demonstrate that adjuvant progestagen therapy improves overall survival or relapse-free survival in women with endometrial cancer. A possible benefit in high-risk subgroups and in cancer-specific survival warrants further investigation, but must be weighed against a potential increase in non-cancer deaths.

## 6. Quality Assessment
- **Study 1:** Quality rating — moderate
  - Strength: Large sample size (n=856)
  - Strength: Multicentre RCT with risk-stratified randomisation
  - Strength: Long follow-up (84 months)
  - Strength: Intention-to-treat analysis
  - Limitation: No effect measures (HR/RR) reported
  - Limitation: p-values not provided, limiting assessment of precision
  - Limitation: No blinding described
  - Limitation: Adverse events not reported
  - Limitation: Complex comparator (radiotherapy ± MPA) may confound intervention effect
  - Bias concern: Incomplete outcome reporting (no p-values or confidence intervals)
  - Bias concern: Potential confounding due to risk-stratified comparators
  - Bias concern: No information on allocation concealment or blinding
  - Applicability: direct
- **Study 5:** Quality rating — moderate
  - Strength: Large sample size (n=1148)
  - Strength: RCT with clear comparator (no additional therapy)
  - Strength: Median follow-up of 72 months
  - Strength: Some outcome p-values reported
  - Strength: Subgroup analysis for high-risk patients
  - Limitation: No effect measures (HR/RR) for survival outcomes
  - Limitation: No blinding mentioned
  - Limitation: Safety signal: higher intercurrent deaths in progestagen group (P=0.04)
  - Limitation: High-risk subgroup analysis may be underpowered (n=461)
  - Bias concern: Possible selective outcome reporting (disease-free survival p-value not reported)
  - Bias concern: No details on allocation concealment
  - Applicability: direct
- **Study 7:** Quality rating — moderate
  - Strength: RCT with adjustment for known prognostic factors
  - Strength: Adequate sample size (n=429)
  - Strength: Over half of patients had >5 years follow-up
  - Limitation: No p-values or effect estimates provided
  - Limitation: Adverse events not reported
  - Limitation: Trial conducted in the 1980s; standard of care may have changed
  - Limitation: Progestogens used as part of primary treatment, not strictly adjuvant post-surgery
  - Bias concern: Outcome reporting limited (only projected 5-year survival)
  - Bias concern: Unclear if assignment was truly random or blinded
  - Bias concern: Potential era-related biases (diagnosis, staging, surgery)
  - Applicability: indirect
- **Study 9:** Quality rating — low
  - Strength: Placebo-controlled, double-blind design
  - Strength: Complete follow-up of all 35 patients at 5 years
  - Limitation: Very small sample (n=35), severely underpowered
  - Limitation: Limited to stage IA/IB only
  - Limitation: Short intervention duration (14 weeks)
  - Limitation: No effect estimates or p-values provided
  - Bias concern: Imprecision due to small sample size
  - Bias concern: Potential publication bias (negative small trial may not have been reported if not for review)
  - Bias concern: Outdated staging and treatment protocols
  - Applicability: indirect
- **Study 10:** Quality rating — low
  - Strength: Prospective RCT design
  - Strength: Reported statistically significant survival benefit (P<0.001)
  - Strength: 5-year follow-up
  - Limitation: Single institution, possibly unblinded
  - Limitation: Small sample (n=205) relative to other trials
  - Limitation: No effect measure reported (only p-value)
  - Limitation: Adverse events not reported
  - Limitation: Results conflict with larger multicentre trials
  - Bias concern: High risk of selection and performance bias (single-centre, unblinded)
  - Bias concern: Potential for selective reporting (only survival p-value given)
  - Bias concern: Inconsistency with other evidence suggests possible bias or confounding
  - Applicability: direct

**Overall evidence strength:** weak

**Key limitations:**
- Effect measures (hazard ratios, relative risks) were not reported in any of the studies, preventing quantitative synthesis.
- Adverse events were not systematically reported across trials; only one study provided safety data.
- The only positive trial (Study 10) is at high risk of bias (single-centre, unblinded, small), and its findings are inconsistent with the larger multicentre trials.
- Sample sizes in two trials (Studies 9 and 10) were insufficient to detect plausible treatment effects.
- Heterogeneity in interventions, comparators, and patient risk profiles limits comparability.
- Publication bias cannot be excluded.
- Detailed study characteristics (e.g., allocation concealment, blinding, full statistical methods) were not available from the extracted abstracts, limiting quality assessment.
