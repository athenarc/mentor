# Systematic Review Report

## 1. Review Summary
- **Title:** Cotrimoxazole prophylaxis for opportunistic infections in children with HIV infection
- **Objective:** To assess the effects of routinely administered cotrimoxazole on death and illness episodes in children with HIV infection, and in infants of HIV infected mothers.

## 2. Search Results
- Number of candidate studies found: 10

## 3. Screening Results
- Included studies: 1
- Excluded studies: 9
- Decisions:
  - Study 1: INCLUDE — Double-blind randomized placebo-controlled trial of routine co-trimoxazole prophylaxis in HIV-infected children aged 1–14 years; comparator is placebo; outcomes include mortality. Meets all selection criteria.
  - Study 2: EXCLUDE — Randomized trial but exclusively in adults; population does not meet child/infant criteria.
  - Study 3: EXCLUDE — Randomized/no-therapy comparison but conducted in adults with AIDS (Kaposi's sarcoma); population not children/infants.
  - Study 4: EXCLUDE — Case series in adult men; not randomized; not a prophylaxis trial.
  - Study 5: EXCLUDE — Observational postmortem study in children; no randomized comparison of cotrimoxazole prophylaxis.
  - Study 6: EXCLUDE — Prospective cohort of HIV-exposed/infected children; no randomized prophylaxis intervention.
  - Study 7: EXCLUDE — Prospective observational study; not a randomized or quasi-randomized trial of cotrimoxazole.
  - Study 8: EXCLUDE — Randomized trial but population is adults with tuberculosis; not children/infants.
  - Study 9: EXCLUDE — Narrative/review on mother-to-child transmission; not a randomized prophylaxis trial.
  - Study 10: EXCLUDE — Diagnostic/epidemiologic study in HIV-infected children; prophylaxis status observational; no randomized comparison.

## 5. Synthesis & Conclusions
Population, setting, and PICO: One randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in Zambia enrolled children aged 1–14 years with clinical features of HIV infection (n=534 HIV-positive after exclusions) in a resource-poor setting with high background bacterial resistance to cotrimoxazole [1]. Participants were randomized to daily cotrimoxazole prophylaxis versus placebo. Median follow-up was 19 months, with intention-to-treat analyses; the trial was stopped early on DSMB recommendation.

Primary outcomes—mortality and illness episodes: Cotrimoxazole significantly reduced all-cause mortality compared with placebo (HR 0.57, 95% CI 0.43–0.77; p=0.0002) [1]. Absolute event rates reported in the abstract were 28% (74 deaths) in the cotrimoxazole arm versus 42% (112 deaths) in the placebo arm over a median of 19 months, indicating a clinically large mortality benefit. Among participants followed >12 months, the mortality effect persisted (HR 0.48, 95% CI 0.27–0.84) [1]. Effects were consistent across age groups (heterogeneity p=0.82) and baseline CD4 strata (heterogeneity p=0.36), suggesting robustness across baseline risk subgroups [1]. Regarding illness episodes, the abstract reported very low identification of Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia (PCP): only one detected case in the placebo arm among 73 nasopharyngeal aspirates obtained from pneumonia cases [1]. Detailed counts for other opportunistic or common infections (e.g., pneumonia, diarrhea, malaria) and hospitalizations were not reported in the abstract.

Adverse events and harms: Overall grade 3/4 adverse event rates were similar between arms (6% vs 7%) [1]. Severe neutropenia (<0.5 × 10^9/L) occurred more often in the cotrimoxazole arm (6% vs 3%), with a borderline p-value (p=0.06) [1]. No clear signal for other serious toxicities emerged in the abstract. The trial occurred in a setting with high background bacterial resistance to cotrimoxazole, yet still showed mortality benefit; however, emergence or selection of antimicrobial resistance during prophylaxis was not directly assessed or reported [1].

Heterogeneity and applicability: As only one trial was included, between-study heterogeneity cannot be assessed. Within-trial subgroup analyses suggested consistent effects across age and CD4 strata [1]. The setting was a low-resource context with high bacterial resistance, making the findings highly applicable to similar environments where HIV-infected children face high burdens of bacterial and opportunistic infections. The study directly addresses routine cotrimoxazole prophylaxis in HIV-infected children; it does not provide evidence for infants of HIV-infected mothers who are HIV-uninfected, so applicability to that subgroup remains uncertain.

Internal validity considerations: Strengths include randomization, double-blinding, placebo control, and ITT analyses [1]. Important limitations include early stopping (which can inflate effect sizes), unclear allocation concealment, and incomplete reporting of attrition and illness/hospitalization outcomes in the abstract [1]. On balance, risk of bias is judged as unclear to moderate.

Conclusions and implications: Moderate-certainty evidence from one RCT in a high-burden, resource-limited setting indicates that routine daily cotrimoxazole prophylaxis substantially reduces all-cause mortality in HIV-infected children [1]. Serious adverse events were comparable between groups, with a borderline increase in severe neutropenia. Data on specific illness episodes, hospitalizations, and antimicrobial resistance were limited or not reported. Routine cotrimoxazole prophylaxis appears justified for HIV-infected children in similar settings, while additional research should clarify effects on specific morbidity outcomes, hospitalization rates, resistance patterns, and the net benefit in infants of HIV-infected mothers.

## 6. Quality Assessment
- **Study 1:** Quality rating — moderate
  - Strength: Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled design (reduces performance and detection bias).
  - Strength: Intention-to-treat analysis reported.
  - Strength: Clinically important primary outcome (all-cause mortality) with large, precise effect size (HR 0.57, 95% CI 0.43–0.77).
  - Strength: Effects consistent across age and baseline CD4 strata (no subgroup heterogeneity reported).
  - Strength: Conducted in a resource-poor setting with high background bacterial resistance, enhancing relevance to similar contexts.
  - Limitation: Allocation concealment details not described in the abstract.
  - Limitation: Trial stopped early by the DSMB, which may overestimate treatment effects.
  - Limitation: Incomplete reporting of denominators and dosing regimen in the abstract.
  - Limitation: Limited reporting on illness episodes (e.g., pneumonia, diarrhea, malaria) and no hospitalization outcomes in the abstract.
  - Limitation: Attrition details and handling of missing data not explicitly described in the abstract.
  - Limitation: Selective outcome reporting cannot be excluded from abstract alone.
  - Limitation: Borderline higher severe neutropenia in the cotrimoxazole arm (6% vs 3%; p=0.06).
  - Limitation: No explicit assessment of antimicrobial resistance emergence reported.
  - Bias concern: Randomization process described but allocation concealment unclear.
  - Bias concern: Early stopping may introduce bias toward larger effects.
  - Bias concern: Attrition and selective reporting domains unclear due to limited reporting in abstract.
  - Bias concern: Outcome measurement likely blinded (double-blind), reducing detection bias.
  - Applicability: direct

**Overall evidence strength:** moderate

**Key limitations:**
- Single RCT constitutes the evidence base; no replication across settings or time.
- Early stopping may overestimate effect size.
- Incomplete reporting in the abstract for allocation concealment, attrition, and illness/hospitalization outcomes.
- No direct assessment of antimicrobial resistance emergence.
- No included evidence for infants of HIV-infected mothers who are HIV-uninfected.
