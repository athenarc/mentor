## 4. Evidence Extraction Summary
**Study 1** — Bwakura-Dangawa et al., 2003 (CHAP Trial)
- **Population:** Children aged 1-14 years with clinical features of HIV infection in Zambia. 534 children included after excluding 7 children who were identified as HIV negative from the original 541 randomly assigned.
- **Setting:** Zambia - area with high levels of bacterial resistance to cotrimoxazole, resource-poor setting
- **Intervention:** Daily cotrimoxazole (trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole) prophylaxis
- **Comparator:** Placebo
- **Outcomes:**
  - All-cause mortality: HR 0.57 (95% CI 0.43–0.77); p=0.0002
  - Mortality in children followed up beyond 12 months (n=320): HR 0.48 (95% CI 0.27–0.84)
  - Grade 3 or 4 adverse events
  - Neutrophil count <0.5x10^9/L; p=0.06
- **Adverse events:** Grade 3 or 4 adverse events: 16 (6%) in co-trimoxazole group vs 18 (7%) in placebo group. Specific events: rash (1 placebo), neutrophil count <0.5x10^9/L on one occasion: 16 (6%) co-trimoxazole vs 7 (3%) placebo, p=0.06
- **Study design:** Double-blind randomised placebo-controlled trial (RCT)
- **Follow-up:** Median 19 months
- **Key findings:**
  - The trial was stopped early in October 2003 by the data and safety monitoring committee due to clear benefit
  - 541 children randomly assigned, 534 analyzed (7 excluded as HIV negative)
  - Mortality: 74 (28%) died in co-trimoxazole group vs 112 (42%) in placebo group
  - Hazard ratio for mortality: 0.57 (95% CI 0.43-0.77, p=0.0002)
  - Benefit consistent across all ages (p=0.82), baseline CD4 counts (p=0.36), and beyond 12 months follow-up (HR 0.48, 95% CI 0.27-0.84)
  - No significant difference in serious adverse events between groups
  - Pneumocystis carinii identified in only 1 placebo patient out of 73 nasopharyngeal aspirates from children with pneumonia
- **Notes:** This is the CHAP (Children's Cotrimoxazole Prophylaxis) trial. The trial was stopped early due to clear mortality benefit. Results suggest children of all ages with clinical features of HIV infection should receive co-trimoxazole prophylaxis in resource-poor settings, irrespective of local resistance to this drug.


## 5. Synthesis & Conclusions
Quality assessment completed for 1 study (Bwakura-Dangawa et al., 2003 CHAP Trial). The study is rated HIGH quality with LOW risk of bias. Cotrimoxazole prophylaxis reduces all-cause mortality by 43% in HIV-infected children (HR 0.57, 95% CI 0.43-0.77) without significant increase in serious adverse events. Evidence strength: MODERATE (limited by single study source).
