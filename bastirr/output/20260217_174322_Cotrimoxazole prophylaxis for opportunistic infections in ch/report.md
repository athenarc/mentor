1. Review Summary
- Title: Cotrimoxazole prophylaxis for opportunistic infections in children with HIV infection
- Objective: To assess the effects of routinely administered cotrimoxazole on death and illness episodes in children with HIV infection, and in infants of HIV‐infected mothers.

2. Search Results
- Candidate studies found: 10

4. Evidence Extraction Summary
- Included study(ies): 1
  • Study 1 PICO:
    - Population: Children aged 1–14 years with clinical features of HIV infection in Zambia (resource-poor, high bacterial resistance setting).
    - Intervention: Daily co-trimoxazole (trimethoprim–sulfamethoxazole) prophylaxis.
    - Comparator: Placebo (double-blind).
    - Setting: Zambia; double-blind randomized placebo-controlled trial; resource-poor setting with high levels of bacterial resistance to co-trimoxazole.
  • Outcomes:
    - All-cause mortality: HR 0.57 favoring co-trimoxazole | 95% CI 0.43–0.77 | p=0.0002 | Follow-up: Median 19 months | Notes: Deaths: 74 (28%) co-trimoxazole vs 112 (42%) placebo. Trial stopped early on DSMC recommendation; intention-to-treat analysis.
    - All-cause mortality beyond 12 months: HR 0.48 favoring co-trimoxazole | 95% CI 0.27–0.84 | Follow-up: Beyond 12 months (n=320) | Notes: Test for heterogeneity by follow-up duration p=0.60.
    - Grade 3/4 adverse events (any): 6% vs 7% (co-trimoxazole vs placebo) | Follow-up: During trial follow-up | Notes: Events: 16 in co-trimoxazole vs 18 in placebo.
    - Severe neutropenia: 6% vs 3% (co-trimoxazole vs placebo) | p=0.06 | Follow-up: During trial follow-up | Notes: Counts: 16 (6%) co-trimoxazole vs 7 (3%) placebo.

5. Synthesis & Conclusions
- Narrative synthesis: In a double-blind randomized placebo-controlled trial in Zambia of HIV-infected children aged 1–14 years [1], daily co-trimoxazole prophylaxis reduced all-cause mortality (HR favoring with 95% CI 0.43–0.77, p=0.0002; median follow-up median 19 months). Deaths were 74/264 (28%) with co-trimoxazole vs 112/267 (42%) with placebo (ARR 14%, NNT ≈ 7) [1]. Benefit persisted beyond 12 months (HR favoring with 95% CI 0.27–0.84) [1]. No significant heterogeneity by age; effect was consistent across age groups [1]. No significant heterogeneity by baseline CD4; effect was consistent across CD4 strata [1]. Safety: Grade 3/4 adverse events were similar (6% vs 7% (co-trimoxazole vs placebo)). Severe neutropenia was numerically higher with co-trimoxazole (6% vs 3% (co-trimoxazole vs placebo)) (p=0.06). Rash events were rare (0 vs 1 case (co-trimoxazole vs placebo)). [1].
- Conclusions: Co-trimoxazole prophylaxis meaningfully reduces all-cause mortality in HIV-infected children (ages 1–14) with an acceptable safety profile in resource-limited settings; evidence for infants of HIV-infected mothers is lacking [1].

6. Quality Assessment
- Risk of bias: Overall low-to-moderate risk of bias: robust randomization and double-blinding with ITT analysis; however, early stopping on DSMC recommendation may overestimate benefit; single-trial evidence.
- Certainty of evidence: moderate
- Applicability: Findings are most applicable to HIV-infected children (1–14 years) in resource-limited settings with high bacterial disease burden and antimicrobial resistance; applicability to infants and high-resource settings is uncertain.
- Limitations:
  • Single RCT; no independent replication
  • Early trial termination may inflate effect size
  • Limited data on rare or long-term adverse events
  • Conducted in one country (Zambia); generalizability may be limited
  • Evidence does not include infants of HIV-infected mothers
- Recommendations for practice/research:
  • Implement routine co-trimoxazole prophylaxis for HIV-infected children where bacterial infections are prevalent, with blood count monitoring for neutropenia.
  • Conduct confirmatory multi-country RCTs including infants of HIV-infected mothers and diverse epidemiologic contexts.
  • Assess long-term safety (hematologic toxicity), antimicrobial resistance, and impacts on non-fatal illness episodes.
  • Standardize adverse event reporting and include patient-important outcomes.
